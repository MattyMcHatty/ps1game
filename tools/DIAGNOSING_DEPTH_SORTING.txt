==============================================================================
 DIAGNOSING DEPTH SORTING   "the wall behind it cuts through in streaks"
==============================================================================

Written after the September 2026 Outside Catacombs fix, where the black backing
grid behind the catacomb mouth sliced through the two door leaves in streaks
whenever the player looked at the doorway off-axis. Head-on it looked perfect.

>>> THE ONE-LINE LESSON: A SORT THAT IS CORRECT HEAD-ON AND WRONG AT AN ANGLE
    IS NOT A NEAR-TIE. IT IS A SIGN INVERSION, AND NO AMOUNT OF EXTRA OT
    RESOLUTION WILL FIX IT. <<<

Companion docs:
  tools/DIAGNOSING_FRAME_RATE.txt  - the other "it looks wrong" runbook
  tools/ADDING_A_ROOM.txt          - room geometry, view distance, culling
  LEVEL_POLYGON_GUIDELINES.txt     - the art-side rule from section 6 below
  src/render.h                     - poly_is_flat_y / otz_far3 / otz_far4


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. WHAT THE HARDWARE ACTUALLY GIVES YOU
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is no Z-buffer. There is one ordering table, and a polygon's slot in it
comes from gte_avsz3/avsz4 — the AVERAGE depth of its corners. One number for
the whole polygon. That is the entire depth facility on this machine.

InitGeom sets ZSF3=341 / ZSF4=256, which makes

    OTZ = avg(SZ) / 4          i.e. 4 world units per OT bucket

and with OT_LENGTH 2048 that is 8192 units of range. The deepest room in this
game culls at 3500 (GC_CULL_DIST), so roughly a quarter of the table is in use.

>>> THIS IS NOT AN SDK SETTING YOU HAVE MISCONFIGURED. <<< PSn00bSDK offers
gte_avsz3/4, ClearOTagR and addPrim. It has no sub-polygon depth facility
anywhere in it, because the hardware has none. Do not go looking through the
SDK for the switch. There isn't one.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. THE FAILURE MODE, IN ONE EQUATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Centroid sorting is not a depth test. Two polys sort correctly only when the
difference in their CENTROID depths has the same sign as the difference in
their REAL depths at the pixels where they overlap.

View-space depth of a point is

    viewz = (X - camx) * sin(theta) + (Z - camz) * cos(theta)

so for two polys being compared:

    dviewz = dx * sin(theta)  +  dz * cos(theta)
              ^ LATERAL            ^ the separation you actually built

The lateral term is the whole bug. It grows with view angle and has nothing to
do with depth. Once |dx * sin(theta)| exceeds dz * cos(theta) the sort flips,
and the flip angle is

    theta_flip = atan(dz / dx)

Read that as: a prop standing dz in front of a wall survives only out to the
angle where its lateral offset from the wall poly overtakes dz. A small dz and
a wide wall is a bug waiting for the player to turn their head.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. WHY IT READS AS "STREAKING" AND NOT AS "THE PROP DISAPPEARS"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every (prop poly, wall poly) pair has its own dx, so every pair has its own
flip angle. As the player rotates, they cross those angles one at a time and
the wall punches through in strips rather than all at once.

The Outside Catacombs numbers, which are worth keeping as a worked example.
Leaf front faces at z=3787, centroids x=-375 and x=-125. Backing grid at
z=3850, cell centroids x=-300, 0, +300. So dz = 63 throughout:

    leaf poly x    backing cell x     dx      flips at
    -----------    --------------   -----     --------
       -125             -300         -175      19.8 deg
       -375             -300          +75      40.1 deg
       -125                0         +125      26.7 deg

63 units of build separation against 175 units of lateral offset. That is the
whole story. Note the pair that fails FIRST is not the one with the largest dx
— pairs only matter if they also overlap on screen, which section 5 covers.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. TIES DO NOT GO TO CHANCE. THEY GO TO WHOEVER DREW FIRST.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

addPrim pushes to the HEAD of the bucket list, and DrawOTagEnv walks from
ot[OT_LENGTH-1] down. So within a single OT slot the LATER-added primitive is
drawn FIRST and gets painted over by the earlier one.

Room draws queue the mesh before they call the prop draws. Therefore:

    >>> AT dotz == 0 THE ROOM WINS AND THE PROP LOSES, EVERY TIME. <<<

The zero-crossing is not a coin flip you might get away with. It is a
guaranteed loss for the prop, which is why the artifact appears the moment the
margin reaches zero rather than somewhere past it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. THE FIX: AN OBJECT-LEVEL OT BIAS, SIZED OFF THE WORST OVERLAPPING PAIR
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Give the prop explicit priority over the surface it is mounted on instead of
letting it argue per-poly on centroid merit. This is what shipped PS1 games
did, and there is precedent in this repo already:

    src/sml_med.c        SML_MED_OT_BIAS 24   (96 units, vs co-located crates)
    src/catacomb_doors.c CD_OT_BIAS      64   (256 units, vs the facade)
    src/vampire.c        otz -= 3
    src/zombie.c         otz += 2

SIZING IT. Only pairs that OVERLAP ON SCREEN can fight, and that bounds dx:
the prop poly's shadow on the wall plane is offset by only dz*tan(theta), so

    max overlapping dx ~= (prop poly half-width)
                        + (wall poly half-width)
                        + dz * tan(theta)

For the catacomb leaves that is 125 + 150 + 63*tan(theta) = 275 + 63*tan(theta),
and the error to beat is

    (275 + 63*tan(t)) * sin(t) - 63 * cos(t)
        = 101 units at 30 deg
        = 195 units at 45 deg
        = 301 units at 60 deg

256 units (64 buckets) covers past 45 degrees and most of the way to 60. Past
60 the prop is nearly edge-on and covers almost no pixels, which is why the
series is allowed to diverge rather than being chased to 90.

>>> CLAMP TO SCENE_OT_MIN AT THE USE SITE. THIS IS NOT DECORATION. <<< Every
other draw loop in this game only ever ADDS to otz, so the uniform +40 kept
them clear of OT slots 0-15 — the menu's reserved range — for free. A negative
bias does not. A prop seen from close up lands in single digits and starts
fighting the inventory UI, in a room nowhere near the one you were fixing.
The shape is:

    otz += 40 - PROP_OT_BIAS;
    if (otz < SCENE_OT_MIN)   otz = SCENE_OT_MIN;
    if (otz >= OT_LENGTH - 1) otz = OT_LENGTH - 2;

WHAT IT COSTS. The bias applies unconditionally, so the prop now draws in front
of ANYTHING inside that slab, not just the wall it was fighting. Before picking
a number, ask what else can stand in those N units — an enemy that wanders
there will sink into the prop. For the catacomb mouth the answer was "nothing
spawns in the approach", which is what made 256 safe.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6. THE ART-SIDE RULE, WHICH IS THE ONE THAT PREVENTS IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The bias is a repair. The cause was building a door 63 units in front of a
900-unit-wide plane.

    >>> IF YOU WANT THE SORT CORRECT OUT TO 45 DEGREES, YOU NEED dz >= dx FOR
        ANY PAIR THAT CAN OVERLAP ON SCREEN. <<<

In practice, for a prop against a wall:

  - Set the backing surface much deeper. 300+ units buys you 60 degrees on the
    same geometry that failed at 20.
  - Better: do not model a plane behind a prop at all. Cut the hole and let the
    hardware clear show through.
  - Best of all, where it applies: do not DRAW the backing surface while the
    prop completely covers it. The catacomb backing grid is 15 prims that are
    invisible whenever the leaves are shut. Skipping them removes the fight and
    saves the prims. (Not done for the catacombs, because the leaves slide open
    and hit the grey facade with the same dz — but reach for it first when the
    prop never moves.)

Subdividing large polys helps, but less than you would hope: it shrinks each
wall cell's dx, and diminishing returns arrive quickly against a small dz while
the prim count keeps climbing. Fix dz first.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 7. WHAT DOES NOT WORK, SO YOU DO NOT SPEND A DAY ON IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RAISING OT RESOLUTION. You could switch to OTZ = avg/2 and stay inside
OT_LENGTH at current cull distances. It will not help. This failure is a SIGN
INVERSION — at 25 degrees off-axis the wall is genuinely NEARER by centroid, by
20-odd units. Doubling the resolution just resolves the wrong answer more
precisely. Keep the idea in your pocket for a genuine near-tie somewhere else.

REACHING FOR poly_is_flat_y / otz_far4. The machinery in src/render.h beats the
lateral term for HORIZONTAL polys by sorting them on their farthest corner,
which is what stopped floors bleeding through the walls standing on them. There
is no equivalent trick for two VERTICAL planes facing the same way. For those
it is explicit priority (section 5) or geometry (section 6), and nothing else.

BLAMING THE EXPORTER OR THE WINDING. Both were checked. A sort inversion looks
nothing like a winding bug — a backwards winding vanishes under gte_nclip at
ALL angles, where this is correct head-on and degrades smoothly as you turn.
If turning the camera changes it, it is depth, not winding.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 8. FAST TRIAGE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Does it look right head-on and wrong at an angle?   -> this document.
     Wrong at every angle?                               -> winding or culling.

  2. Dump the two meshes and get dz and dx. tools/check_model_winding.py has a
     working SMD loader to import (load() returns verts and prims); 20 lines on
     top of it prints per-poly centroids and bounds.

  3. Compute atan(dz/dx) for the pairs that overlap on screen. If it lands near
     the angle where you SEE the artifact, the diagnosis is confirmed and you
     can stop looking.

  4. Apply section 5. Check section 6 for whether the geometry should change
     too, so the next prop in that room does not repeat it.
