PS1 HORROR GAME — DIAGNOSING A FRAME-RATE DROP: THE COMPLETE RUNBOOK
====================================================================
Written immediately after Maze One was dragged back from 30fps to 60fps
(Aug 2026). It records the method that eventually worked, the instrument that
had to be built before anything could be measured, and — at greater length,
because they cost more time than the fix did — the four wrong turns taken on
the way, every one of them an optimisation written before anything was measured.

>>> THE ONE-LINE LESSON: THREE OF THE FOUR OPTIMISATIONS WRITTEN BY REASONING
    ABOUT THIS ROOM WERE WORTHLESS, AND ONE OF THEM MADE IT 56% WORSE. THE TWO
    THAT WORKED WERE BOTH WRITTEN AFTER A MEASUREMENT SAID WHERE TO LOOK. <<<

Companion docs:
  tools/ADDING_A_ROOM.txt        - the room runbook; view distance and culling
  tools/ADDING_AN_ENEMY.txt      - per-frame AI cost lives here
  tools/TEXTURING_NOTES.txt      - VRAM, texture windows
  rebuild commands.txt           - build steps


##########################################################################
 STEP 0 — THE INSTRUMENT. DO NOT SKIP THIS.
##########################################################################
Press SELECT in game. It cycles eight levels:

    0  off
    1  PERF METER ONLY  <- read every number here
    2  authoring overlay: coordinates, compass, held keys
    3  + collision visualisation
    4  perf meter + room mesh not drawn
    5  perf meter + the room's frustum cull off (an A/B slot; repoint it at
       whatever cull is currently on trial)
    6  perf meter + view distance 2000
    7  perf meter + view distance 1800

Level 1 reads:

    FPS60 VB1
    4808B ~120p
    U43 D232 G2
    T31474

  VB    frame period in whole vblanks. 1 = 60fps, 2 = 30fps.
  B/p   packet-buffer bytes queued, and bytes/40 as a primitive estimate
        (POLY_FT4 is 40 bytes — an older meter divided by 28 and overstated
        every count in this room by 40%).
  U     hblanks in update_current_area  — the game's logic
  D     hblanks queueing the scene      — CPU, and where culling work lands
  G     hblanks WAITING at DrawSync     — the GPU's overhang
  T     the raw hblank counter, so a dead timer is obvious at a glance

>>> 262 HBLANKS IS ONE 60Hz FRAME. <<< U + D + G under 262 is 60fps. The three
numbers therefore read almost as percentages of a frame, and — this is the whole
point — they SIZE a problem instead of merely detecting one.

  G near zero  = CPU-bound. Fill is not your problem, whatever it looks like.
  G large      = GPU/fill-bound. Now, and only now, culling pixels is worth it.

Maze One at its worst read U43 D365 G2. G2 of 262. Every optimisation aimed at
fill was aimed at 0.8% of the frame.


##########################################################################
 STEP 1 — BISECT WITH THE ISOLATION SWITCHES, NOT WITH GUESSES
##########################################################################
Levels 4-7 remove one part of the scene each. Read VB and D at each and the
answer falls out in one sitting rather than one rebuild per hypothesis. In Maze
One the four readings were:

    no mesh      VB1  39p   <- the room's GEOMETRY is the cost
    no sprites   VB2 279p   <- the ENEMIES are not, however many there are
    half cull    VB1 112p
    empty        VB1  39p

Add a switch for anything you are about to spend an afternoon on. They cost
about ten lines each (DEBUG_EXPERIMENT() in collision.h, honoured in the room's
draw) and they are the difference between knowing and believing.


##########################################################################
 STEP 2 — READ THE RIGHT NUMBER
##########################################################################
Three separate metrics lied in this investigation. All three are still on the
meter, because each is useful for something; none of them is the frame cost.

1. VB IS TOO COARSE TO OPTIMISE AGAINST. VB2 covers everything from 16.7ms to
   33.3ms. Maze One went from 410 hblanks to 277 across several changes and VB
   read "2" the whole way — a 32% improvement that was completely invisible.
   USE VB TO DETECT, U/D/G TO MEASURE.

2. "GPU-bound" ON THE OLD METER WAS NOT A BOTTLENECK TEST. perf_gpu_busy is
   sampled BEFORE the DrawSync, so it reports "the GPU is still working through
   the previous frame" — true of any non-trivial scene, at 60fps as well as 30.
   It said GPU-bound in every single reading taken in this room, including the
   ones running at full rate. It is a liveness flag, not a verdict. G is the
   verdict.

3. PRIMITIVE COUNT IS NOT FRAME COST. Measured in this room: 93 primitives ran
   at VB2 while 112 primitives ran at VB1. Reception draws ~951 and is fine.
   What a primitive costs depends on how big it is on screen and what the CPU
   had to do to decide to send it — the count on its own predicts neither.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3 — WHAT ACTUALLY FIXED IT
##########################################################################
Both winners were CPU, and both were found by measuring first.

A. A GUNFIRE-SHAPED FUNCTION ON THE PER-FRAME AI PATH.
   collision_segment_blocked() samples its segment every 30 units and calls nine
   prop families at each sample. Its own comment justified that with "firing is
   infrequent, so the extra samples are free" — but src/mushroom.c calls it up to
   four times a FRAME per enemy, and the sample count scales with the segment's
   LENGTH. A mushroom's pacing sightline runs the full length of its patrol leg,
   so Maze One's 6097-unit leg alone cost 203 samples x 9 prop calls x 60 fps in
   a room containing no props whatsoever.
   FIX: a *_any_solid() predicate per prop family, aggregated by props_any_solid()
   in collision.c, gating the whole sampling pass. A prop-less room (every garden
   room — chainlink doors and levers exist only in the Attic Exit) collapses to
   the exact wall-crossing loop. Cost only; answers unchanged.

B. THE REJECT PATH WAS TOUCHING THE MESH.
   Only ~120 of Maze One's 2037 primitives survive the distance cull, so ~1900 a
   frame are pure overhead — and each one still cost a read of the primitive
   header for its stride, a read of its first vertex INDEX, and a chase into the
   117 KB vertex array for the coordinates. The R3000 has NO DATA CACHE behind
   any of that and the mesh is far too big for the scratchpad, so every one was a
   main-memory stall.
   FIX: mo_keys[] in maze_one.c — a 12 KB table built once at load holding each
   primitive's first-vertex X/Z and its stride. A rejected primitive now costs
   one sequential 6-byte read and never touches the mesh. Identical output; it
   changes what the reject path READS, not what it decides. Hoisting isin/icos
   and the cull distance out of the loop went in at the same time.

   >>> THIS IS THE FIRST THING TO TRY IN ANY ROOM WITH A BIG MESH. <<< It is
   behaviour-neutral, it scales with primitives REJECTED rather than drawn, and
   it is the reason this room ended up back at 60fps.


##########################################################################
 STEP 4 — THE WRONG TURNS. ALL FOUR WERE WRITTEN BEFORE MEASURING.
##########################################################################
1. HEDGE OCCLUSION CULLING — A NET LOSS OF HALF A FRAME.
   Reasoning: 68% of the polys inside the frustum are behind another hedge, so
   skipping them saves the GPU filling pixels it then paints over. All of that
   was TRUE and all of it was IRRELEVANT: G was 2. It cost 133 hblanks a frame
   of sightline arithmetic (D went 232 -> 365) to save fill nobody was short of.
   Deleted. If a room is ever genuinely fill-bound, the code is in the git
   history and the measurements were sound — it was the premise that was wrong.

2. SIDE-PLANE FRUSTUM CULLING — EXACTLY NEUTRAL.
   Removes 72% of submitted primitives (measured offline against the mesh, zero
   holes) and D was IDENTICAL with it on and off: 232 either way. It saves one
   GTE transform per culled poly and spends the same amount deciding. Kept,
   because it halves packet-buffer use and leaves GPU headroom for free — but it
   fixed nothing, and a room already in trouble should not expect it to.

3. THE DOUBLE SCREEN CLEAR — REAL WASTE, WRONG HALF OF THE MACHINE.
   The draw environments carry isbg=1, so DrawOTagEnv already fills the whole
   framebuffer before a primitive is drawn; every room then queued its OWN
   full-screen TILE on top to get its own colour. That is a second 77k-pixel fill
   per frame, about a millisecond, for free. render_set_clear_colour() (render.h)
   replaces it and Maze One now uses it. Worth doing — but it is GPU time, VB did
   not move, and 18 other rooms still paint their own tile.

4. SPRITE OCCLUSION CULLS ON THE ENEMIES — UNMEASURED, STILL IN.
   The Rafflesia's mist and body and the Mushroom Head's sprite are all skipped
   when a wall stands between them and the camera (collision_hidden_from_camera).
   Same premise as (1) and the same objection applies: they spend CPU sightlines
   to save fill. They are small and they are still in place, but they have never
   been measured and by this document's own argument they may be a slight net
   loss. MEASURE BEFORE DEFENDING THEM.

And one that wasted a round on its own: THE FIRST SECTION TIMER DID NOT RUN.
SetRCnt()/StartRCnt() need a BIOS event opened and enabled first; called alone
they leave the counter dead and the meter reads U0 D0 G0. Drive the hardware
directly, as the SDK's own example does (share/psn00bsdk/examples/system/timer):

    TIMER_CTRL(1) = 0x0100;              /* hblank source, free-run, no IRQ */
    ticks = TIMER_VALUE(1) & 0xFFFF;     /* wraps every 4.2 s; deltas & 0xFFFF */

The T field on the meter exists so this can never silently happen again.


##########################################################################
 STEP 5 — THE ORDER TO WORK IN, NEXT TIME
##########################################################################
  1. Read U/D/G at level 1 in the worst spot in the room. Write them down.
  2. G large?  -> fill. Attack overdraw, view distance, sprite sizes, blending.
     G small?  -> CPU. Attack the draw loop's reject path and the AI.
  3. Bisect with the isolation switches before writing a line of optimisation.
  4. Where the mesh is implicated, do the cull-key table (STEP 3B) FIRST — it is
     behaviour-neutral and it is usually the biggest single win.
  5. Check U as well as D. A room's logic can be a sixth of its frame; Maze One
     sat at U43 of 262 and that was never investigated because the fix landed
     first. The monsters were "exonerated" early on the VB meter, which could
     not resolve 43 hblanks — that exoneration was drawn with a broken
     instrument and would need re-testing before it is relied on.
  6. Re-measure after EVERY change. Two of the four wrong turns above would have
     been caught by one reading.

  And an honest note on the emulator: these numbers came from PCSX-Redux, whose
  GPU timing is not necessarily faithful to real hardware. It hardly mattered
  here because the answer was CPU-bound and CPU timing is accurate — but a
  FILL-bound verdict from an emulator is worth confirming on hardware or in a
  second emulator before reshaping any geometry around it.
