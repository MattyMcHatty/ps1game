PS1 HORROR GAME — DIAGNOSING A FRAME-RATE DROP: THE COMPLETE RUNBOOK
====================================================================
Written immediately after Maze One was dragged back from 30fps to 60fps
(Aug 2026). It records the method that eventually worked, the instrument that
had to be built before anything could be measured, and — at greater length,
because they cost more time than the fix did — the four wrong turns taken on
the way, every one of them an optimisation written before anything was measured.

>>> THE ONE-LINE LESSON: THREE OF THE FOUR OPTIMISATIONS WRITTEN BY REASONING
    ABOUT THIS ROOM WERE WORTHLESS, AND ONE OF THEM MADE IT 56% WORSE. THE TWO
    THAT WORKED WERE BOTH WRITTEN AFTER A MEASUREMENT SAID WHERE TO LOOK. <<<

Companion docs:
  tools/ADDING_A_ROOM.txt        - the room runbook; view distance and culling
  tools/ADDING_AN_ENEMY.txt      - per-frame AI cost lives here
  tools/TEXTURING_NOTES.txt      - VRAM, texture windows
  rebuild commands.txt           - build steps


##########################################################################
 STEP 0 — THE INSTRUMENT. DO NOT SKIP THIS.
##########################################################################
Press SELECT in game. It cycles nine levels:

    0  off
    1  PERF METER ONLY  <- read every number here
    2  authoring overlay: coordinates, compass, held keys
    3  + collision visualisation
    4  perf meter + room mesh not drawn
    5  perf meter + the room's frustum cull off (an A/B slot; repoint it at
       whatever cull is currently on trial)
    6  perf meter + view distance 2000
    7  perf meter + view distance 1800
    8  perf meter + the room's ENEMIES, WATER AND PROPS not drawn
    9  perf meter + the enemy AI FROZEN (the only switch that moves U)

Level 8 is 4's mirror image and was added for the Greenhouse's flood (see the
GREENHOUSE section at the bottom). D read at 4 and at 8 splits the draw section
between the mesh and everything standing in it, in one sitting. Maze One's
original "no sprites" reading was taken on the VB meter, which STEP 2 shows
could not resolve 43 hblanks — level 8 is that reading done with an instrument
that can. Maze One honours 4-7; the Greenhouse honours all five.

>>> LEVEL 9 IS THE ONLY ONE THAT MOVES U. <<< Every switch above it changes what
is DRAWN, so all of them leave the game's logic exactly where it was. 9 freezes
the area-tagged enemy AI everywhere, and U read at 1 against U at 9 is the first
number anyone has ever had for "how much of the frame is the monsters". It makes
the game unplayable while it is on; that is the point.

Level 1 reads:

    FPS60 VB1
    4808B ~120p
    U43 D232 G2
    T31474

  VB    frame period in whole vblanks. 1 = 60fps, 2 = 30fps.
  B/p   packet-buffer bytes queued, and bytes/40 as a primitive estimate
        (POLY_FT4 is 40 bytes — an older meter divided by 28 and overstated
        every count in this room by 40%).
  U     hblanks in update_current_area  — the game's logic
  D     hblanks queueing the scene      — CPU, and where culling work lands
  G     hblanks WAITING at DrawSync     — the GPU's overhang
  T     the raw hblank counter, so a dead timer is obvious at a glance

>>> 262 HBLANKS IS ONE 60Hz FRAME. <<< U + D + G under 262 is 60fps. The three
numbers therefore read almost as percentages of a frame, and — this is the whole
point — they SIZE a problem instead of merely detecting one.

  G near zero  = CPU-bound. Fill is not your problem, whatever it looks like.
  G large      = GPU/fill-bound. Now, and only now, culling pixels is worth it.

Maze One at its worst read U43 D365 G2. G2 of 262. Every optimisation aimed at
fill was aimed at 0.8% of the frame.


##########################################################################
 STEP 1 — BISECT WITH THE ISOLATION SWITCHES, NOT WITH GUESSES
##########################################################################
Levels 4-7 remove one part of the scene each. Read VB and D at each and the
answer falls out in one sitting rather than one rebuild per hypothesis. In Maze
One the four readings were:

    no mesh      VB1  39p   <- the room's GEOMETRY is the cost
    no sprites   VB2 279p   <- the ENEMIES are not, however many there are
    half cull    VB1 112p
    empty        VB1  39p

Add a switch for anything you are about to spend an afternoon on. They cost
about ten lines each (DEBUG_EXPERIMENT() in collision.h, honoured in the room's
draw) and they are the difference between knowing and believing.


##########################################################################
 STEP 2 — READ THE RIGHT NUMBER
##########################################################################
Three separate metrics lied in this investigation. All three are still on the
meter, because each is useful for something; none of them is the frame cost.

1. VB IS TOO COARSE TO OPTIMISE AGAINST. VB2 covers everything from 16.7ms to
   33.3ms. Maze One went from 410 hblanks to 277 across several changes and VB
   read "2" the whole way — a 32% improvement that was completely invisible.
   USE VB TO DETECT, U/D/G TO MEASURE.

2. "GPU-bound" ON THE OLD METER WAS NOT A BOTTLENECK TEST. perf_gpu_busy is
   sampled BEFORE the DrawSync, so it reports "the GPU is still working through
   the previous frame" — true of any non-trivial scene, at 60fps as well as 30.
   It said GPU-bound in every single reading taken in this room, including the
   ones running at full rate. It is a liveness flag, not a verdict. G is the
   verdict.

3. PRIMITIVE COUNT IS NOT FRAME COST. Measured in this room: 93 primitives ran
   at VB2 while 112 primitives ran at VB1. Reception draws ~951 and is fine.
   What a primitive costs depends on how big it is on screen and what the CPU
   had to do to decide to send it — the count on its own predicts neither.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3 — WHAT ACTUALLY FIXED IT
##########################################################################
Both winners were CPU, and both were found by measuring first.

A. A GUNFIRE-SHAPED FUNCTION ON THE PER-FRAME AI PATH.
   collision_segment_blocked() samples its segment every 30 units and calls nine
   prop families at each sample. Its own comment justified that with "firing is
   infrequent, so the extra samples are free" — but src/mushroom.c calls it up to
   four times a FRAME per enemy, and the sample count scales with the segment's
   LENGTH. A mushroom's pacing sightline runs the full length of its patrol leg,
   so Maze One's 6097-unit leg alone cost 203 samples x 9 prop calls x 60 fps in
   a room containing no props whatsoever.
   FIX: a *_any_solid() predicate per prop family, aggregated by props_any_solid()
   in collision.c, gating the whole sampling pass. A prop-less room (every garden
   room — chainlink doors and levers exist only in the Attic Exit) collapses to
   the exact wall-crossing loop. Cost only; answers unchanged.

B. THE REJECT PATH WAS TOUCHING THE MESH.
   Only ~120 of Maze One's 2037 primitives survive the distance cull, so ~1900 a
   frame are pure overhead — and each one still cost a read of the primitive
   header for its stride, a read of its first vertex INDEX, and a chase into the
   117 KB vertex array for the coordinates. The R3000 has NO DATA CACHE behind
   any of that and the mesh is far too big for the scratchpad, so every one was a
   main-memory stall.
   FIX: mo_keys[] in maze_one.c — a 12 KB table built once at load holding each
   primitive's first-vertex X/Z and its stride. A rejected primitive now costs
   one sequential 6-byte read and never touches the mesh. Identical output; it
   changes what the reject path READS, not what it decides. Hoisting isin/icos
   and the cull distance out of the loop went in at the same time.

   >>> THIS IS THE FIRST THING TO TRY IN ANY ROOM WITH A BIG MESH. <<< It is
   behaviour-neutral, it scales with primitives REJECTED rather than drawn, and
   it is the reason this room ended up back at 60fps.


##########################################################################
 STEP 4 — THE WRONG TURNS. ALL FOUR WERE WRITTEN BEFORE MEASURING.
##########################################################################
1. HEDGE OCCLUSION CULLING — A NET LOSS OF HALF A FRAME.
   Reasoning: 68% of the polys inside the frustum are behind another hedge, so
   skipping them saves the GPU filling pixels it then paints over. All of that
   was TRUE and all of it was IRRELEVANT: G was 2. It cost 133 hblanks a frame
   of sightline arithmetic (D went 232 -> 365) to save fill nobody was short of.
   Deleted. If a room is ever genuinely fill-bound, the code is in the git
   history and the measurements were sound — it was the premise that was wrong.

2. SIDE-PLANE FRUSTUM CULLING — EXACTLY NEUTRAL.
   Removes 72% of submitted primitives (measured offline against the mesh, zero
   holes) and D was IDENTICAL with it on and off: 232 either way. It saves one
   GTE transform per culled poly and spends the same amount deciding. Kept,
   because it halves packet-buffer use and leaves GPU headroom for free — but it
   fixed nothing, and a room already in trouble should not expect it to.

3. THE DOUBLE SCREEN CLEAR — REAL WASTE, WRONG HALF OF THE MACHINE.
   The draw environments carry isbg=1, so DrawOTagEnv already fills the whole
   framebuffer before a primitive is drawn; every room then queued its OWN
   full-screen TILE on top to get its own colour. That is a second 77k-pixel fill
   per frame, about a millisecond, for free. render_set_clear_colour() (render.h)
   replaces it and Maze One now uses it. Worth doing — but it is GPU time, VB did
   not move, and 18 other rooms still paint their own tile.

4. SPRITE OCCLUSION CULLS ON THE ENEMIES — UNMEASURED, STILL IN.
   The Rafflesia's mist and body and the Mushroom Head's sprite are all skipped
   when a wall stands between them and the camera (collision_hidden_from_camera).
   Same premise as (1) and the same objection applies: they spend CPU sightlines
   to save fill. They are small and they are still in place, but they have never
   been measured and by this document's own argument they may be a slight net
   loss. MEASURE BEFORE DEFENDING THEM.

And one that wasted a round on its own: THE FIRST SECTION TIMER DID NOT RUN.
SetRCnt()/StartRCnt() need a BIOS event opened and enabled first; called alone
they leave the counter dead and the meter reads U0 D0 G0. Drive the hardware
directly, as the SDK's own example does (share/psn00bsdk/examples/system/timer):

    TIMER_CTRL(1) = 0x0100;              /* hblank source, free-run, no IRQ */
    ticks = TIMER_VALUE(1) & 0xFFFF;     /* wraps every 4.2 s; deltas & 0xFFFF */

The T field on the meter exists so this can never silently happen again.


##########################################################################
 STEP 2A — >>> G IS NOT THE VERDICT ONCE VB IS ALREADY 2. <<<
##########################################################################
Added after the Rabisu fight (Aug 2026), which was diagnosed twice from the
wrong number because STEP 2's rule is stated more strongly than it holds.

"G near zero = CPU-bound" is true at VB1. At VB2 IT IS A FALSE NEGATIVE, and
the reason is in the definition: G is the wait at DrawSync for the PREVIOUS
frame's GPU work. A dropped frame hands the GPU a whole spare vblank of wall
clock, so it finishes during the VSync wait and the CPU never waits for it. The
meter then reads G0 — "the GPU is idle" — about a frame the GPU is setting the
length of.

THE READING THAT EXPOSED IT. Two isolation levels in the Garden Courtyard came
back with the same numbers and different frame rates:

    level 4  (no room mesh, boss only)   U4 D118 G0    LAGS
    level 8  (no boss, room only)        U5 D117 G0    FINE

Identical U, D and G cannot produce different frame rates, so the cost was
somewhere none of the three was looking. It was pixels: 526 room primitives that
are mostly small and distant against 476 boss primitives that are large and
close. Same primitive count, same CPU cost, wildly different fill.

  >>> SO ALWAYS RECORD FPS/VB ALONGSIDE U/D/G. <<< STEP 2 says "use VB to
  DETECT, U/D/G to MEASURE" and that is right — but a U/D/G reading with no VB
  beside it cannot be interpreted at all, because you cannot tell whether G0
  means "the GPU is idle" or "the GPU had a free vblank to hide in".

  THE FREE TEST FOR FILL, when G cannot be trusted: MOVE THE CAMERA. Fill scales
  with the screen area a thing covers; CPU cost per primitive does not care.
  Smooth from across the arena and choppy up close is pixels, every time.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3C — WHAT FIXED THE RABISU FIGHT (Aug 2026)
##########################################################################
Reported as "it lags the whole time the boss is in the level", where it used to
lag only during certain attacks. Three changes, in the order they were made;
the first two took the fight itself to a clean 60 and the third was aimed at
what was left.

1. isin/icos HOISTED OUT OF THE COURTYARD'S REJECT LOOP. Two SDK calls per
   primitive, 1052 a frame, to compute one pair of constants that cannot change
   while a frame is being queued. This is the tail of STEP 3B — the room never
   got it because it predates that work.

2. THE DOUBLE SCREEN CLEAR REMOVED (wrong turn #3). The room queued its own
   full-screen TILE over a framebuffer DrawOTagEnv had already cleared: a second
   77,000-pixel fill every frame, for the colour alone. render_set_clear_colour.

3. THE LAWN LIGHTS' BACK WALLS CULLED. What was left was the REVEAL only — the
   one moment with sixteen light columns on screen. Each is a pool quad plus a
   shaft drawn as a CLOSED BOX of four walls in three bands: 13 additive quads
   per light, 208 in total, all semi-transparent and heavily overlapping.

   Two things that look like savings there and are not:
     - CUTTING THE BAND COUNT saves primitives and not one pixel. The bands
       exist for the GTE's +/-1023 clamp; the shaft covers the same screen area
       whether it is three quads or one.
     - SKIPPING DIM QUADS. The unlit cells are already skipped, and the shaft's
       height falloff bottoms out around 13% rather than at black, so there is
       nothing contributing zero.

   >>> WHAT DID WORK: BACKFACE-CULLING THE SHAFT. <<< Every camera sees two of a
   box's four walls from in front and two from behind. Culling an ADDITIVE quad
   is not free the way culling a solid one is — an additive back face is not
   hidden by the front face, it adds through it — so this genuinely removes
   light and has to be paid for: RBS_SHAFT_LEVEL went 60 -> 120, and two walls
   at 120 sum through the middle of a beam to the same 240 four walls at 60 did.
   Half the shaft's pixels for a look one constant restores.

   THE REMAINING LEVERS, if a future effect needs more, all of them pure fill
   and all of them a LOOK decision rather than a code one: RBS_SHAFT_H (900),
   RBS_SHAFT_FLARE (150), and the number of cells lit at once.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3A, PART TWO — THE GREENHOUSE (Aug 2026)
##########################################################################
The Greenhouse's flood — six Rafflesias, four Mushroom Heads, five vine curtains
and a 60-droplet water pool, dropped into the largest room in the game — was
reported as lag, and the first question was whether Maze One's two winners
applied. They half did, and the half that did not is the interesting part.

WHAT WAS ALREADY THERE. The Greenhouse was built after Maze One was fixed and
inherited every mesh-side change wholesale: the cull-key table (gh_keys, STEP 3B),
the side-plane frustum cull, the hoisted trig and cull distance, and
render_set_clear_colour instead of a second full-screen fill. There was nothing
of STEP 3B left to do — do check this before assuming, but a room written after
August 2026 probably has it.

WHAT DID NOT APPLY, AND WHY. STEP 3A's props_any_solid() gate is a BOOLEAN, and
a boolean only ever helps a room with NO props: it skips the sampling pass
entirely in the gardens, the mazes and Fountain Square. The Greenhouse holds vine
curtains, so the gate passes and the sampler runs — and then paid for ELEVEN prop
families at every sample point to find the one family that could possibly answer.

That had never mattered before, because until the flood no room combined the two
halves: the rooms with props had no sprite enemies doing per-frame sightlines,
and the rooms with sprite enemies had no props. The Greenhouse is the first room
that is both, and it is also the largest, so its segments are the longest:
collision_hidden_from_camera runs from the camera to each of ten sprites, up to
the room's 2500 fog distance, i.e. up to 83 samples apiece, plus the Mushroom
Heads' own alert and chase sightlines.

THE FIX: props_any_solid() became props_solid_mask(), one bit per family,
computed once per segment and carried into props_block_point. The inner loop goes
from eleven calls to one in this room. Behaviour-neutral by the same argument the
boolean gate already relied on — a family whose *_any_solid() is false cannot
have a *_point_solid() return true, because point_solid tests the same
active/state/area gates and then some. Every other room is unaffected sample for
sample.

>>> THE GENERAL LESSON, WHICH IS THE SAME ONE AS BEFORE. <<< A guard that asks
"is there any work here at all" is worth writing, and it is worth checking
whether it is asking the question at the right granularity. Maze One needed
"none"; the Greenhouse needed "which". The same loop, the same fix, one level
down.

THE READINGS, taken in the flooded nave:

    level 1 (everything)   U60  D210  G1     271 of 262 -> VB2
    level 4 (no mesh)      U50  D30   G1
    level 8 (no entities)  froze the game -- see THE FIFTH WRONG TURN below

G1. CPU-bound, exactly as Maze One was; fill is not the problem however much
water is on screen. And D falls from 210 to 30 with the mesh left out, so THE
MESH IS 180 OF THE 210 and everything the flood added — ten sprites, six
curtains, sixty droplets, the prompts — is the other 30. The room was over by
nine hblanks, which is 3.4%.

>>> THE POPULATION WAS NOT THE COST, AND THE PROP-MASK FIX ABOVE WAS AIMED AT
THE WRONG THIRD OF THE FRAME. <<< It went in before any of these numbers
existed, on the strength of the reasoning in the section above, and the reasoning
was sound but the target was U — which is 60, a fifth of the frame and not what
put the room over. It is kept because it is behaviour-neutral and it does make
that fifth cheaper, but it is the fifth wrong turn's cousin: written before a
measurement, and lucky rather than right. The measurement said MESH.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3B, PART TWO — THE GREENHOUSE'S FRUSTUM TEST WAS STILL READING THE MESH
##########################################################################
With D=180 in the mesh loop, the obvious question is how much it is drawing.
Counted offline against Greenhouse.smx over 16 headings from the middle of the
nave (the tool is a twenty-line script; write one, do not estimate):

    1230 primitives walked      the whole mesh, gh_keys only, 6 bytes each
     573 pass the distance cull the room is OPEN. Nearly half the mesh is within
                                2500 of the player from anywhere in the nave —
                                where Maze One's hedges keep it to ~120 of 2056
     296 ENTER THE SIDE-PLANE TEST'S VERTEX LOOP
     276 of those 296 are thrown away
     122 actually drawn

The drawn count is fine — 122 is the same order as Maze One at VB1. The cost was
the middle line. STEP 3B lifted the DISTANCE key out of the mesh, and then the
frustum test put the mesh back on the reject path one test later: ~296 primitives
a frame reading v1..v3 out of a 69 KB vertex array, scattered, with no data cache,
and 93% of them discarded.

FIX: a second key, gh_box[] — each primitive's XZ BOUNDING BOX — tested against
the side planes instead of its vertices.

  IT IS NOT AN APPROXIMATION. The plane predicate is LINEAR in (x,z), so over a
  convex box its minimum is at a corner: all four corners outside a plane PROVES
  every vertex is outside it. Hole-free by construction, whatever the mesh —
  which is a stronger guarantee than the vertex loop's, since that one was only
  verified against one mesh at one moment. Checked anyway: 3,845,254 primitive/
  pose combinations over the whole walkable footprint at 32 headings, ZERO holes,
  and it culls 1.1% MORE than the vertex loop did (most of this mesh is
  axis-aligned wall and glazing quads, whose XZ box IS the quad).

  IT IS A SEPARATE ARRAY FROM gh_keys, AND THAT MATTERS. The key is read for all
  1230 primitives every frame; the box is read only by the ~573 that pass the
  distance cull. Folding four more int16s into GhCullKey would have put 8 bytes
  of extra traffic on the hot path to save reads on the cold one.

  ARITHMETIC STAYS IN THE <<12 DOMAIN rather than shifting down first the way
  Maze One's does. >>12 floors, and a floored comparison can go the wrong way by
  a unit at the screen edge — tolerable when it only confirms a per-vertex answer,
  not tolerable when it is a proof about a box.

>>> MAZE ONE HAS NOW HAD IT, AND THE PARAGRAPH THAT USED TO STAND HERE WAS
WRONG. <<< It said the box key should not be ported unmeasured, because Maze
One's vertex loop "runs on ~120 primitives, not 296, because a maze is not an
open room". The first half of that was right and the number was not: it was
taken AT THE GATES, which are the cheap corners of the room. Counted offline
over 16 headings, primitives ENTERING THE VERTEX LOOP:

    west gate spawn    154        maze centre         294
    north gate spawn   153        BIRD CAGE POCKET    329
    east gate spawn    141

329 is MORE than the 296 that justified the box key in the Greenhouse, and 94%
of them were thrown away after chasing v1..v3 through a 118 KB vertex array.
Maze One's mesh is the biggest on the disc; a maze is not an open room, but it
is a room whose middle nobody had put a number on. The box key went in
(src/maze_one.c, mo_box), hole-checked over the whole walkable footprint:
10,897,337 primitive/pose combinations, zero holes, and it culls 0.9% more than
the vertex loop did.

>>> THE LESSON IS THE DOCUMENT'S OWN, TURNED ON THE DOCUMENT. <<< "Measure
before porting it" was the right instruction and it was written next to a
measurement taken in the wrong place. STEP 5 point 1 says to read the numbers
IN THE WORST SPOT IN THE ROOM. That applies to the offline counts as well as to
the meter: sweep the room, do not sample the doorway.


##########################################################################
 THE FIFTH WRONG TURN — AN INSTRUMENT THAT CRASHED THE GAME
##########################################################################
Debug level 8 froze on the frame it was selected. Not the switch: the METER.

    static const char *exp_name[5] = { "", "no mesh", ... };
    ... exp_name[DEBUG_EXPERIMENT()] ...

DEBUG_EXPERIMENT() now returns 5, the array holds 5 entries, and the garbage
pointer went straight into snprintf's %s. Adding a DBG_EXP_* to collision.h and
forgetting its label in main.c's meter is a hang, not a missing string.

The array is sized from DBG_EXP_NO_ENTITIES + 1 now and the index is clamped, so
the next switch added without a label prints blank instead of dying. But the
lesson is the older one from this document, wearing different clothes: the
instrument is code too, and STEP 0 says do not skip it — which is no use if
reaching for it is what breaks the run.


AFTER THE BOX KEY, measured in the same spot:

    level 1  (everything)        U70  D180  G1     251 of 262   <- under budget
    level 5  (frustum cull off)  U60  D176  G1
    level 6  (view distance 2000)U80  D150  G1     231
    level 7  (view distance 1800)U65  D145  G1     211

D 210 -> 180. Thirty hblanks, which is the 296 mesh chases a frame gone, and it
is what took the room from 271 to 251 and back under the 262 budget. That is the
whole of the fix; nothing else moved D.

>>> THE MARGIN IS ELEVEN HBLANKS, AND U SWINGS BY TWENTY. <<< U reads 60, 65, 70
and 80 across the four readings above with nothing in the update path changed
between them, so it is genuinely varying with where the player stands and what
the monsters are doing. Eleven hblanks of headroom against that is not a lot: the
room is at 60fps on average and will still drop the odd frame. THAT is why level
9 exists.

>>> AND THE SIDE-PLANE FRUSTUM CULL IS NOT PAYING FOR ITSELF. <<< D is 180 with
it and 176 WITHOUT it. Maze One measured the same test exactly neutral and kept
it for the packet-buffer headroom; here it is four hblanks on the wrong side of
neutral, and the box key is what made it cheap enough for that to even be close.
DO NOT DELETE IT ON THIS READING ALONE. What it is really buying is packet buffer,
and an overflowing buffer does not slow down — the draw loops silently stop
emitting primitives and geometry vanishes. Read the B/~p line at level 1 and at
level 5 before touching it; if B is comfortably inside BUFFER_LENGTH either way,
it is dead weight and can go.

THE VIEW DISTANCE is the biggest lever left: 1800 buys another 35 hblanks of D
and takes the frame to 211, a 20% margin. It is also the only change here that
alters what the room LOOKS like — greenhouse.h argues the long view down the nave
is the point of the place — so it is a design call, not a performance one, and it
needs a person to look at it rather than a number.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3A, PART THREE — U, AND THE THIRD CONVICTION OF THE SAME FUNCTION
##########################################################################
    level 9 (AI frozen)   U7  D180  G0

>>> SIXTY-THREE OF THE SEVENTY HBLANKS OF U ARE THE ENEMY AI. <<< Ninety per cent
of the game's logic, a quarter of the whole frame, for ten enemies. This is the
number STEP 5 point 5 has been asking for since Maze One, and it took a switch
that moves U to get it — every earlier level only changes what is DRAWN.

WHICH ten. Not the flowers: update_rafflesias is one integer square root and some
arithmetic per instance, no sightline anywhere in it (the Rafflesia's two
collision_hidden_from_camera calls are in its DRAW, so they land in D). That
leaves the FOUR Mushroom Heads holding essentially all of it — about 16 hblanks,
a full millisecond, each, every frame.

And the cost is collision_segment_blocked. Again. Third time in this document.

    if (m->state == MSH_PACE) {
        ...
        int clear = !collision_segment_blocked(m->x, m->y, m->z, wx, m->y, wz);
        msh_steer(m, gx, gz, MSH_WALK_SPEED, clear);

That segment is the full remaining length of the patrol leg — up to 1692 units in
this room, so ~56 samples — and it ran for every mushroom on every frame.

>>> AND THE ANSWER WAS USUALLY NEVER READ. <<< Trace what `clear` does inside
msh_steer: it zeroes `blocked` and `steer_timer`, and the only two branches that
read those are already dead when both are zero. So on any frame where the feeler
probe hit nothing and no wall-follow commit was running — which is most frames,
for a mushroom patrolling an open aisle — the trace was computed and then changed
nothing whatsoever.

FIX: msh_steer now takes the sightline's TARGET rather than its ANSWER, and
traces it only inside `if (blocked || m->steer_timer > 0)`. Identical on every
frame by construction, not by measurement:

    blocked == 0 && steer_timer == 0  ->  both branches inert either way, so the
                                          answer cannot be read. Skip.
    otherwise                         ->  the same test, run as before.

The ALERT sightline higher up is untouched and must stay eager: its answer starts
a scream, so it is consulted the instant it is taken.

>>> src/spider.c HAS THE SAME SHAPE AND HAS NOT BEEN CHANGED. <<< It is not in
the room anyone measured, and the whole of this document is about not doing that.

>>> AND A FOURTH CONVICTION, ONE LEVEL BELOW ALL THREE. <<< walls_crossed() —
the wall half of collision_segment_blocked, and the only half that runs in a
prop-less room — tested EVERY wall in the room with six multiplies and a
sign-normalise. A crossing point lies inside both segments and therefore inside
both bounding boxes, so a wall whose XZ box is disjoint from the segment's
cannot be crossed: four comparisons, exact, removes work and never an answer.
Counted offline over Maze One's footprint at 8 headings with a 1200-unit
sightline: 304,000 wall tests, 6,960 survive the box. 97.7% of the loop now
costs two compares instead of six multiplies, in every room, and it is the
first of these four fixes that helps the rooms with no props at all — which is
every garden room, the mazes and Fountain Square, i.e. everywhere the sampler
gate already collapses to this loop and this loop alone.

>>> THE PATTERN, THREE TIMES OVER. <<< Every one of this function's three
convictions was the same mistake in a different coat: work sized for a rare
event (a gunshot) sitting on a path walked every frame.

    Maze One    the sampler ran in a room with no props at all   -> gate it
    Greenhouse  the sampler asked eleven families to find one    -> mask it
    Greenhouse  the answer was computed and then not read        -> defer it

None of the three changed a single answer. If there is a fourth, expect it to be
the same shape.


    level 1, after the deferred sightline   U35  D180  G1     216 of 262

U 70 -> 35, and the lag in the measured spot is gone.


##########################################################################
 STEP 6 — WHEN THE CODE IS DONE AND THE ROOM IS STILL HEAVY
##########################################################################
The Greenhouse ended at U35 D180 G1 in the nave, and D still climbs past 200 in
some parts of the room. At that point the question stops being "what is the code
doing wrong" and becomes "is this simply too much room for the machine".

>>> IT IS NOT. AND THE WAY TO KNOW THAT IS TO COUNT THE MESH, NOT TO GUESS. <<<
STEP 2 point 3 already says primitive count is not frame cost — Reception draws
~951 at full rate. The Greenhouse draws between 122 and 211. Nothing about what
is ON SCREEN is near this machine's limit. What was over the limit is how many
primitives the room is DESCRIBED with: 1230, walked in full every frame.

So count what those 1230 actually are. Grouping every wall quad by texture and
XZ footprint, and every floor quad by grid cell:

    740 wall quads          sitting in only 225 vertical COLUMNS
                            105 of those columns are FIVE quads tall
    490 floor quads         a plain ~200 x 200 grid over 4500 x 5300
      -- no other geometry to speak of

>>> TEN BUTTONS CUT THE WHOLE ROOM INTO FIVE HORIZONTAL STRIPS. <<< The wall
bands are y[-900,-675], [-675,-450], [-450,-243], [-243,-158] and [-158,0], and
that fourth one — 113 quads of it, right round the room — is the height of the
pipe buttons in greenhouse_puzzle.c. There are TEN buttons. A loop cut made to
place ten quads was carried around the entire shell, and it and the band above it
account for most of the 740.

None of that subdivision carries any detail. A 200 x 200 floor tile is flat and
identical to its neighbour; a wall column of five stacked strips is one wall.

    merge each wall column (contiguous strips only)   740 -> 242   saves 498
    merge the floor 2x2                               490 -> 140   saves 350
    ------------------------------------------------------------------------
    1230 -> ~358 primitives, and the room looks the same

UV headroom is not the constraint: a 200-unit tile spans ~34 texels of the 128
window, and POLY_FT4's UVs are uint8, so a merged quad has room for seven tiles
per axis before it overflows 255. WARPING is the constraint — the PS1 has no
perspective-correct texturing, so a big polygon swims. That is almost certainly
why the grid is there in the first place, and it is why the two merges are not
equally safe:

  WALL COLUMNS ARE THE SAFE ONE and the bigger win. A wall is seen close to
  face-on from standing height, which is where affine warping is least visible,
  and the merge is purely vertical — the horizontal 200-unit spacing stays. 498
  primitives, 40% of the mesh, for very little risk.

  THE FLOOR IS THE RISKY ONE. Floors are seen at grazing angles, which is
  exactly where warping shows. Do the walls first, measure, and only go to the
  floor if it is still needed — and then 2x2, not 4x4.

>>> RE-EXPORTING MOVES EVERY PRIMITIVE INDEX. <<< Three things are keyed by
index and all three must be redone in the same sitting:
    src/greenhouse_tex_map.h   regenerate: py gen_greenhouse_tex_map.py
    greenhouse_nocull[]        same generator, same file
    BUTTON[].prim in src/greenhouse_puzzle.c   RE-DERIVE, do not nudge. That
        file already says so: the check is that exactly ten prims carry slot 9
        in the tex map and the same ten appear in the table.
Collision is generated from a SEPARATE mesh ("Greenhouse mesh.smx") and is not
affected, so the room's walls and floors do not move.

THE ALTERNATIVE, IF THE MESH IS NOT TO BE TOUCHED, is view distance: 1800 was
measured at D145 against 180. It costs the long view down the nave, which
greenhouse.h argues is the point of the room. That is a design decision and it
wants a person looking at the screen, not a number.

>>> AND THE GENERAL RULE THIS ROOM ADDS. <<< Before removing anything from a
level, count how many primitives its geometry is DESCRIBED with against how many
it NEEDS. Grid subdivision left over from modelling is invisible on screen and
costs the whole frame, every frame — it is always the first thing to spend, and
it is cheaper than any content decision.


>>> WHAT HAS STILL NOT BEEN MEASURED. <<<
  the B/~p line, at levels 1 and 5, which decides the frustum cull's fate (D is
      180 with it and 176 without, so it is currently dead weight unless it is
      protecting the packet buffer).
  VB/FPS at level 1, to confirm what U+D+G already says.
  the worst spots themselves. Swept offline, D peaks around the middle of the
      nave looking WEST — (-2100,-1600) and (-1700,-1800) at cam_rot ~3072 draw
      211 and 204 against the 122 typical of the nave centre. Stand there with
      the meter up before and after any mesh change.


##########################################################################
 STEP 5 — THE ORDER TO WORK IN, NEXT TIME
##########################################################################
  1. Read U/D/G at level 1 in the worst spot in the room. Write them down.
  2. G large?  -> fill. Attack overdraw, view distance, sprite sizes, blending.
     G small?  -> CPU. Attack the draw loop's reject path and the AI.
  3. Bisect with the isolation switches before writing a line of optimisation.
  4. Where the mesh is implicated, do the cull-key table (STEP 3B) FIRST — it is
     behaviour-neutral and it is usually the biggest single win.
  5. Check U as well as D. A room's logic can be a sixth of its frame; Maze One
     sat at U43 of 262 and that was never investigated because the fix landed
     first. The monsters were "exonerated" early on the VB meter, which could
     not resolve 43 hblanks — that exoneration was drawn with a broken
     instrument and would need re-testing before it is relied on.
  6. Re-measure after EVERY change. Two of the four wrong turns above would have
     been caught by one reading.

  And an honest note on the emulator: these numbers came from PCSX-Redux, whose
  GPU timing is not necessarily faithful to real hardware. It hardly mattered
  here because the answer was CPU-bound and CPU timing is accurate — but a
  FILL-bound verdict from an emulator is worth confirming on hardware or in a
  second emulator before reshaping any geometry around it.
