PS1 HORROR GAME — ADDING A NEW PROP: THE RUNBOOK
================================================
The procedure for adding a static or puzzle prop (a mesh that stands in a
room, collides, and may carry a "Press O to ..." sign). Written after Sharur's
Tablet (Sharur's Trial, Nov 2026), whose first build shipped two mistakes this
file now exists to stop: a standoff that let the eye inside the stone, and a
sign that sat on the face and disappeared into it. Those two are STEP 4 and
STEP 5, and they apply to EVERY prop, not only tablets.

Reference implementations (copy the nearest one):
  src/sharur_tablet.c   a slab at an ANGLE: oriented box measured at load,
                        examine shot, hit counter, shatter, pickup spawn
  src/gula_tablet.c     an axis-aligned slab, box measured off its mesh
  src/crib.c            a breakable box prop, reach measured to its SURFACE
  src/sconce.c, src/oil_dispenser.c   simple boxes measured off the SMD

Companion docs:
  tools/MESH_CONVERSION_NOTES.txt   smxlink, not python, for the visual mesh
  tools/TEXTURE_BANKS.txt           declare the bank (since streaming a short
                                    mask only misplaces a PINNED texture)
  src/texmgr.h STREAMING            a mid-room texmgr_upload() must texmgr_pin()
  tools/VRAM_MAP.txt / VRAM_MAP_UNDERWORLD.txt   where its textures may go
  tools/ADDING_AN_ITEM.txt          if the prop leaves a collectible behind
  tools/ADDING_A_SOUND.txt          if it needs a sound the room's bank lacks
  LEVEL_POLYGON_GUIDELINES.txt      props against walls: depth-sort gaps
  rebuild commands.txt              build + disc, after every change

NOTE ON PYTHON: `python` on PATH is the Store stub. Use `py`.


##########################################################################
 STEP 1 — THE MESH
##########################################################################
Have the prop exported IN PLACE (world coordinates, from the room's .blend) to
assets/props/<Name>.smx, and convert with smxlink:

    smxlink -tp textures/<chapter dir> -o assets/props/<name>.smd "assets/props/<Name>.smx"

Exported in place, the prop's position, facing and size are all IN the mesh:
measure them at load from the vertices (sharur_tablet.c sh_measure) rather
than typing coordinates in. Check the poly winding against the export's
normals before trusting a cross product (Sharur's: outward = (v2-v0)x(v1-v0)).


##########################################################################
 STEP 2 — TEXTURES, VRAM, BANK
##########################################################################
4bpp TIMs via textures/png_to_tim.py, a FREE page from the chapter's VRAM map,
a free CLUT line, and the right bank (texmgr_set_bank before texmgr_register).
Upload them from the room's *_upload_textures() through a NARROW uploader so
tools/gen_tex_rooms.py can see the call. Regenerate the VRAM map(s) and run
py tools/check_tex_banks.py: "No unexpected pixel collisions" / "OK".


##########################################################################
 STEP 3 — DISC, BUILD, LOAD
##########################################################################
The .smd and .tims go in disc.xml under the chapter's directory; the .c in
CMakeLists.txt. Run py tools/heap_budget.py afterwards - a full heap crashes
at boot.

WHERE THE SMD LIVES. A prop only ONE room draws should not sit on the heap
for the whole run: read it on room entry into the ROOM ARENA's tail with
room_arena_load_extra() (src/room_arena.h), from the room's *_load_geometry
AFTER its room_data_bind, and trust the pointer only while
room_arena_generation() still matches the one noted at the load - the next
room's load overwrites it. Zero heap. The Lightning Tablet
(src/lightning_tablet.c) is the reference; it handed back 6 KB that a
startup read_file would have held forever. A startup load is still right for
a prop several rooms draw (the save point, the sconce).

>>> A HEAP-HELD PROP READS THROUGH cd_read_file() (src/cd_file.h). <<< Not a
private malloc(sectors * 2048) + CdRead: that holds the tail of the last
sector for as long as the model is held - a quarter of every prop buffer,
25 KB across the game, until November 2026. Keep the module's static
read_file()/load_file() as a one-line wrapper round it (copy src/crib.c's):
tools/heap_budget.py finds the loads by those names and recognises the
wrapper as exact-size.

>>> AND ITS SMD SHIPS WITHOUT NORMALS. <<< Add its disc name to ALLOWLIST in
tools/pack_models.py and point its disc.xml source= at
...\ps1game\build\models\<path below assets\> instead of assets\ (the build
writes it). That drops the normals array (~28% of the file) that no prop draw
reads. ONLY IF ITS DRAW READS NORMALS - it calls smdSortModel (crate.c), or
reads p_norms / the p+12 normal index (weapon.c, helluminator.c, sharur.c) -
does it stay on assets\ and go in NEEDS_NORMALS instead. pack_models.py fails
the build if a new source file starts reading normals, so the two lists cannot
quietly drift. tools/RAM_AB_CHECKLIST.txt PART B.


##########################################################################
 STEP 4 — COLLISION OFF THE PROP'S OWN DIMENSIONS: THE STANDOFF
##########################################################################
>>> THE PROP'S OWN BOX IS NOT ENOUGH. HOW FAR THE PLAYER IS HELD OFF IT
    (THE RADIUS PASSED TO *_collide) DECIDES WHETHER THEY CAN SEE INTO IT. <<<

Measuring the box off the mesh gives the prop's SURFACE. The radius you push
the player out by is the gap between that surface and the EYE. Most props get
75 - fine for a waist-high crate, WRONG for anything tall and wide that the
player can walk flat up against:

  Sharur's Tablet, 1200 long x 600 tall x 100 thick, at 75: the face's
  corners projected past the GTE's +/-1023 screen guard, those polys were
  dropped by the draw loop's reject, and the player "poked through" - the
  stone vanished from around them. Fixed by holding them 195 off it (the
  wall standoff), which keeps the whole face on screen.

RULE OF THUMB. With gte_SetGeomScreen(256), a vertex at depth D and sideways
(or vertical) offset S lands S*256/D pixels off centre; past ~1023 its poly is
rejected. So a face whose far corners are S from the eye needs

        standoff  >=  S / 4      (S*256/D <= 1023)

  - S is the larger of half the face's width and the face's height above or
    below the eye (eye is at floor - GROUND_FLOOR_Y - 40 = 189 above the floor).
  - 75 covers S up to ~300: crates, stands, small tanks. Fine.
  - Anything with a face wider than ~600 or taller than ~500 the player can
    stand square against: use the WALL standoff (195), as the Incinerator
    and Sharur's Tablet do, or the prop's own as the Cleaver's does.

CHECKLIST for a prop collided off its own dimensions:
  [ ] Worked out S for its biggest face and picked the radius from it.
  [ ] Passed a NAMED constant (SH_COLLIDE_RADIUS, CL_COLLIDE_RADIUS), with the
      reason written next to it - not a bare 75 copied from the line above.
  [ ] Re-checked every reach measured from the player against the new
      standoff: interact reach, melee reach (measure to the SURFACE, the
      crib's rule, or a long face is unreachable at its ends), pickup spawn.
      Each must exceed the standoff with room to spare.
  [ ] If the prop stands within ~2x the standoff of a wall or rock, collide
      it BEFORE the wall loop in collision.c (the statues', Hadad's and
      Sharur's Tablet's place), so the hard geometry has the last word and
      the bigger push cannot shove the player into the wall behind.
  [ ] Walked flat into every face, both ends, and the back, in the emulator.


##########################################################################
 STEP 5 — THE SIGN ("Press O to examine")
##########################################################################
>>> THE SIGN HOVERS IN FRONT OF THE FACE. IT NEVER SITS ON IT. <<<

door_draw_string_3d_yaw places the text at the point you give it, in the
plane of the yaw. Put that point on (or 20 off) the prop's face and the text
lies flat on the texture at the stone's own depth: green on a busy carving,
it read as part of the carving and was missed. Sharur's Tablet's first sign
was at 20; it is now at 100.

  [ ] Sign point = face centre + outward normal * ~100 (SH_SIGN_OUT), i.e.
      clearly off the face (several times the prop's half-thickness)...
  [ ] ...and still INSIDE the collision standoff (100 < 195), so the player
      can never walk into or through the letters.
  [ ] Yaw = the direction the READER faces, not the face normal.
  [ ] Height: the room's door-sign height (door signs at -200 in Ch4).
  [ ] Shown only while it can be acted on (standing, not being read, player
      in front of the face), faded over the same 1000-1500 as door signs.


##########################################################################
 STEP 6 — WIRING
##########################################################################
  - room upload_textures:  the prop's narrow uploader
  - room init:             *_arm() (per-visit state; flags are read live)
  - room draw:             inside the DBG_EXP_NO_ENTITIES gate, BEFORE
                           item_pickups_draw if the prop's draw leaves the
                           view in the GTE for a sign
  - collision.c:           *_collide (STEP 4 for the radius and the order)
  - main.c:                *_update in the room's block; a cutscene branch at
                           the top of update_current_area if it takes the
                           camera; re-derive from flags after a load
  - player.h:              game flags APPENDED at the end of their enum
  - debug_opts / title.c:  a "<PUZZLE> SOLVED" grant if it is a puzzle


##########################################################################
 STEP 7 — VERIFY
##########################################################################
  [ ] cmake --build build --target horror_game, then mkpsxiso -y disc.xml
      (kill pcsx-redux first; check the .cue is not empty).
  [ ] py tools/check_tex_banks.py, py tools/heap_budget.py.
  [ ] In the emulator: walk into every face (STEP 4), read the sign from the
      front and from an angle (STEP 5), and watch the frame rate approaching
      it from every side - a prop placed against the room's busiest geometry
      can expose overdraw behind it (tools/DIAGNOSING_FRAME_RATE.txt).
