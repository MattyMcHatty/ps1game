PS1 HORROR GAME — ADDING A BOSS ENCOUNTER: THE RUNBOOK
=======================================================
An ENEMY is a thing in a room. An ENCOUNTER is a thing that happens TO the
player: the camera is taken away, something is revealed, music starts, a fight
is fought inside a sealed arena, and a death sequence plays. This file is the
runbook for the second thing. It assumes the first is already done.

It was written immediately after the Rabisu encounter was built (August 2026),
the game's first boss fight, and records every step that was actually
performed — including the ten things that were wrong first, which are listed
at the bottom and are the most useful part of the file.

Companion docs, in the order you will want them:
  tools/ADDING_A_3D_ENEMY.txt    - get the BODY in the room first. Nothing here
                                   works until the boss exists, hovers, takes
                                   damage and persists.
  tools/ADDING_AN_ENEMY.txt      - the base enemy runbook underneath that one
  tools/ADDING_A_ROOM.txt        - the arena had better exist too
  tools/AREAS_AND_WORLD_NOTES.txt- the area/state model and world.c

  src/rabisu_boss.c / .h         - THE reference encounter. Read it end to end.
  src/rabisu.c / .h              - the body it drives
  src/lightswitch_puzzle.c       - the reference for CAMERA CUTS and for
                                   ADDITIVE LIGHT (its cones are the ancestor
                                   of the lawn lights)
  src/piano_puzzle.c             - the reference for CAMERA GLIDES and for a
                                   multi-beat scripted payoff


##########################################################################
 HOW TO KICK THIS OFF (what the user provides)
##########################################################################
  1. "Follow tools/ADDING_A_BOSS_ENCOUNTER.txt"
  2. Which boss, and which room is the arena.
  3. The BEAT SHEET, in prose and in seconds. The Rabisu's was one paragraph
     per phase: camera takes over, 2 s of watching, lights come on 2 at a time
     over 3 s, 5 s rise, 2 s fade, music, two lines of text at 6 s each, fight,
     then the death sequence.
  4. The MOVEMENT RULE and the ATTACKS, with damage as a percentage of the
     player's bar and every window in seconds.
  5. Which weapons can hurt it, and which cannot.

Everything else — the arena's bounds, the boss's movement radius, which polys
the lights sit on, where the cameras go — is DERIVED (STEP 2). Do not ask the
user for coordinates.

>>> FOUR THINGS ARE WORTH ASKING ABOUT, BECAUSE NOTHING IN THE REPO ANSWERS
    THEM AND GUESSING WRONG COSTS THE WHOLE ENCOUNTER <<<
  a. "THE CAMERA MOVES TO A SPOT AND CONTROL IS GIVEN BACK" — does the PLAYER
     move there, or does a detached camera watch them from there? In this
     engine the camera IS the player and there is no avatar to render, so a
     detached fight camera would show an empty arena. Confirm it; the answer
     was "the player is moved there", and the whole handover (STEP 3) hangs
     off it.
  b. HIT POINTS, once you have removed a weapon. The Rabisu had 20 HP when the
     crucifaxe did 1 a swing. Taking the axe away made 20 mean "twenty
     Grave-olver rounds" against a scarce ammo economy. It went to 12. State
     the arithmetic and let the user pick.
  c. WHAT HAPPENS IF THE PLAYER LEAVES OR DIES mid-fight. Replay the whole
     reveal? Skip it? Seal the room? The Rabisu seals the room, which is only
     viable because that room has no save point — check that before offering
     it (STEP 9).
  d. WHETHER THE ARENA MUSIC IS THE BOSS'S MUSIC. If it is, it stops being
     room music and the room is silent before and after (STEP 9).


##########################################################################
 STEP 1 — SPLIT THE DIRECTOR FROM THE BODY
##########################################################################
Two files, and the split is not cosmetic.

  src/<slug>.c        THE BODY. Model, animation, damage, collision, and the
                      combat AI: how it moves, what it throws, what its
                      attacks do. All per-instance. Would work unchanged for a
                      second one of these dropped in another room.

  src/<slug>_boss.c   THE ENCOUNTER. Camera, lights, subtitles, the music cue,
                      the sealed door, the phase machine, the death sequence.
                      All one-off script bolted to one room.

They talk through a small named API and nothing else. The Rabisu's is five
functions, declared in a marked-off block at the foot of rabisu.h:

    <Slug> *<slug>_boss_instance(void);   /* the live one here, or NULL      */
    void    <slug>_go_dormant(<Slug> *);  /* park the AI; I'll pose the body */
    void    <slug>_fight_begin(<Slug> *); /* take it, it's yours             */
    void    <slug>_face_override(<Slug> *, int on, x, z);   /* look HERE     */
    void    <slug>_anchor_world(const <Slug> *, lx, ly, lz, VECTOR *out);

plus four plain FIELDS the director writes directly — `frozen`, `shake`,
`fade` and `clip_y` (STEP 5).

The traffic goes the other way too: the shared additive-glow primitives live
in the ENEMY file, because two of their four users are attacks. See STEP 7.

>>> RESIST PUTTING THE CUTSCENE IN THE ENEMY FILE. <<< It is one file either
way on the first boss and it is obviously one file. It stops being obviously
one file on the second, at which point the movement AI and the fireballs are
welded to a script about a specific lawn.


##########################################################################
 STEP 2 — MINE THE ARENA
##########################################################################
The fight's geometry is already in the repo. All of it. Read it out; do not
invent numbers and do not ask for them.

A. THE BOUNDS COME FROM THE COLLISION MESH.
   src/<room>_mesh_collision.c lists every wall with its y_min/y_max and every
   floor plane with its y. A "lip the player can fall down but cannot climb
   back up" is a wall whose y band spans two adjacent floor heights.

   The Garden Courtyard's are walls 0/5 (x = +1142) and wall 6 (x = -1722),
   both y 800..900, separating the y=800 terrace from the y=900 sunken lawn.
   The brief said "3 polys in from the east and west walls"; the terrace strips
   are 858 and 857 wide against a ~286 poly, so three polys IS exactly those
   two x values. The prose and the mesh agreed, which is the check you want.

B. THE MOVEMENT RADIUS FALLS OUT OF THE SPAWN.
   The boss spawns at x = -290 (world.c). To each lip is 1432. Symmetric, and
   not by accident — but it is only true for THAT spawn, so say so next to the
   constant. Move the placement and the radius is a lie.

C. THE LIGHT POLYS COME FROM THE .smx, NOT FROM ARITHMETIC.
   "The central 16 polys of the grassy part" is a real, countable set. Parse
   the mesh and find them:

     py -c "import xml.etree.ElementTree as ET; \
       r=ET.parse('assets/Garden Courtyard.smx').getroot(); \
       t=[x.get('file') for x in r.find('textures').findall('texture')]; \
       v=[(float(a.get('x')),float(a.get('y')),float(a.get('z'))) \
          for a in r.find('vertices').findall('v')]; \
       c=[]; \
       [c.append((min(p[0] for p in q),max(p[0] for p in q), \
                  min(p[2] for p in q),max(p[2] for p in q))) \
        for q in ([v[int(pp.get('v%d'%k))] for k in range(4) \
                   if pp.get('v%d'%k) is not None] \
                  for pp in r.find('primitives').findall('poly') \
                  if pp.get('texture') is not None and \
                     t[int(pp.get('texture'))]=='grss') \
        if set(round(p[1]) for p in q)=={900}]; \
       print(len(c)); print(sorted(set((a,b) for a,b,_,_ in c))); \
       print(sorted(set((a,b) for _,_,a,b in c)))"

   That printed 64 flat quads in an 8x8 grid, columns and rows ~286 apart. The
   4x4 block straddling the spawn is x[-866,283] z[-571,571], whose centre is
   (-291, 0) — the spawn, to within the mesh's own rounding. Sixteen polys,
   and they are the RIGHT sixteen.

   Store the 5 column edges and the 5 row edges as two arrays. Do NOT store a
   cell size and multiply: the mesh's columns are 286, 286, 288, 289 and the
   lights would drift off the grass.

D. THE CAMERA SHOTS ARE COMPUTED, NOT EYEBALLED.
   At gte_SetGeomScreen(256) on a 320x240 screen, the half-field at distance D
   is D*160/256 horizontally and D*120/256 vertically. Pick a distance, work
   out what the frame covers, and check the whole subject is inside it.

   The Rabisu's crane shot, worked in the comment above the constants:
     spawn (-290, 800, 0); model is RBS_HEIGHT (559) tall, so it occupies
     y 800 down to 241 and the shot centres on y~570;
     camera (-290, 200, -1500), yaw 0 (due north: forward is (sin,cos) of
     cam_rot — see update_camera);
     pitch = atan(370/1500) in 4096ths = 158;
     frame at 1500 = y[-133,1273] x[-1227,647], which holds the turf (900),
     the crown (241) and the whole 1149-wide light patch.

   Write that derivation down. The next person to nudge the camera 200 units
   has to redo the pitch, and they will not know that unless you say so.

E. THE HANDOVER SPOT IS A STANDING POSITION AND OBEYS THE ROOM'S RULES.
   Eye height = floor_surface - GROUND_FLOOR_Y - 40 (the standoff apply_height
   applies), pitch 0, and NOT on a wall push-out boundary. The Rabisu's fight
   spot sits 20 short of the room's GC_WALL_RADIUS limit: hand control back ON
   the boundary and the player's first frame of free play is a shove.


##########################################################################
 STEP 3 — THE CAMERA
##########################################################################
Three mechanics, all of which already exist. Use them; do not invent a fourth.

TAKEOVER. Save the camera, then ANCHOR THE PLAYER where they stood:

    save_cx = cam_x; save_cy = cam_y; save_cz = cam_z;
    save_crot = cam_rot; save_cvy = cam_vy;
    camera_anchor_player(save_cx, save_cy, save_cz);

camera.h explains why: the camera flies off, the PLAYER does not, and anything
reading cam_* to find the player would spend the cutscene hunting an empty
patch of garden. See STEP 6's rule about player_x/y/z().

GLIDE. The piano puzzle's ease-out, factored into one helper because an
encounter needs four of them and four inlined lerps is four chances to differ:

    e = 256 - ((256-p)*(256-p) / 256);          /* p = t*256/frames */
    cam_x = src.x + ((dst.x - src.x) * e) / 256;   /* ...y, z, pitch */
    cam_rot = (src.rot + (turn_delta * e) / 256) & 4095;

Interpolate the YAW THROUGH turn_delta() (the shortest signed turn), never by
lerping the raw angle — a glide from 4000 to 100 would otherwise spin the
camera the long way round.

HANDOVER. This is the trick, and it is worth stating plainly:

    glide the camera to the fight spot, then camera_release_player()

Releasing the anchor makes player_x/y/z() read cam_* again — and the camera is
standing at the fight spot. The player is now THERE. That is the whole of "the
camera moves to a spot and control is given back to the player".

Re-anchor to the DESTINATION at the START of that final glide, not at the end.
Otherwise the boss spends the last second tracking the door the player came in
by and then snaps its gaze across the arena the instant control returns.

>>> THE PITCH TRAP. THIS COST A WHOLE SHOT. <<<
Most rooms build their view matrix by hand:

    SVECTOR neg_rot = {0, -cam_rot, 0, 0};      /* YAW ONLY */
    RotMatrix(&neg_rot, &rot_matrix);

That silently DISCARDS cam_pitch. camera.h says so, in as many words. A crane
shot through such a room is not a tilted camera — it is a high camera staring
level at the far wall, and nothing warns you.

Any room that hosts an encounter with a pitched shot must build its view with
camera_build_view(), and SO MUST THE BOSS'S OWN DRAW, which builds its own copy
of the view before composing the model matrix onto it. Miss the second one and
the boss renders through a different projection than the room it stands in.

    MATRIX view;
    camera_build_view(&view);      /* identical output when pitch == 0 */

Both conversions are no-ops for every other frame the player spends in the
room, so there is no reason not to do them.

RESTORE ON THE WAY OUT. Every exit path — the good one and the "the boss
vanished out from under the script" one — must put cam_pitch back to 0 and call
camera_release_player(). Free-look gameplay never sets pitch and will never
clear it for you.

>>> THE BOSS MUST FACE THE CAMERA DURING A CUTSCENE, NOT THE PLAYER. <<<
This one is a direct consequence of the anchor and it will bite you: the whole
enemy layer targets player_x/y/z() (STEP 6, correctly), and during a cutscene
that is a fixed point just inside whatever door the player walked in by. A boss
whose only job is to look impressive therefore hauls itself out of the ground
with its back three-quarters turned to the shot.

Give the entity a facing OVERRIDE the director can switch on, and point it at
the shot's FIXED position rather than the live cam_* — otherwise it swivels
while the camera glides:

    rabisu_face_override(r, 1, RBE_CAM_X, RBE_CAM_Z);   /* reveal AND death   */
    ...
    rabisu_fight_begin(r);                              /* drops it silently  */

Do it for the death sequence too. It plays to the same crane shot, and dying
while staring at the corner the last bullet came from looks like a bug.


##########################################################################
 STEP 4 — THE PHASE MACHINE
##########################################################################
One enum, one `phase_t` frame counter, one switch. Every duration a named
constant in FRAMES with the brief's seconds in the comment, all in one block so
the beat sheet can be read off the header:

    #define RBE_T_CAM_IN        90   /* 1.5 s glide out to the crane shot */
    #define RBE_T_WATCH        120   /* 2 s of nothing, as specified      */
    #define RBE_T_LIGHTS_UP    180   /* 3 s, sixteen lights two at a time */
    ...

Phase changes go through ONE helper that also zeroes the counter:

    static void enter_phase(RbeState s) { state = s; phase_t = 0; }

ARM IT LAZILY. The room's init runs BEFORE world_enter() places the boss, so an
init-time decision about whether there is a boss to reveal is made a frame too
early. <slug>_boss_enter() therefore only PARKS the director, and the first
update that finds a living boss starts the reveal:

    if (state == RBE_IDLE) { if (found) { boss_idx = ...; begin_reveal(found); } return; }

That also gives the "already killed, player walked back in" case for free:
<slug>_boss_instance() skips the dead, so it stays IDLE and nothing happens.

>>> THEN LATCH THE BOSS AS AN INDEX AND NEVER LOOK IT UP AGAIN. <<<
"Find the live boss in the current area" is the right question for ARMING and
the wrong one for DRIVING. It returns NULL in two situations that are both
completely normal, and a script that re-asks it every frame tears itself down
in both:

  - THE FRAME AFTER YOUR OWN FADE SETS `dead`. The "boss vanished, bail out"
    guard then fires BEFORE the final camera move can run — so the camera is
    released while it is still parked at the crane shot, and the player is
    dumped wherever that was instead of being carried back to where they were
    standing. It looks precisely like "it put me back at the start of the
    fight", which sends you hunting through the wrong code.
  - ANY FRAME THE INVENTORY MENU IS OPEN, because game_state becomes
    STATE_MENU and the area tag stops matching. Opening the menu mid-fight
    ended the entire encounter and unsealed the door.

    static int boss_idx = -1;
    static Rabisu *boss(void) {
        if (boss_idx < 0 || boss_idx >= rabisu_count) return NULL;
        Rabisu *r = &rabisus[boss_idx];
        return r->active ? r : NULL;
    }

The index cares about neither game_state nor `dead`. Keep the bail-out — a
debug jump really can empty the slot — but make it test the SLOT, not the
lookup. Clear boss_idx in both _reset() and _enter().

TWO PHASES ARE NOT CUTSCENES. The FIGHT and DONE states are free play. Expose
that distinction, because main.c needs it:

    int <slug>_boss_cutscene(void)   { return state != IDLE && != FIGHT && != DONE; }
    int <slug>_boss_seals_door(void) { return state != IDLE && != DONE; }

BAIL OUT CLEANLY. A debug level-select jump or a load can pull the boss out
from under a running script. If <slug>_boss_instance() returns NULL mid-phase,
release the camera, zero the pitch, go to DONE — do not drive a NULL through
the rest of the switch.


##########################################################################
 STEP 5 — POSING THE BODY: FOUR FIELDS AND TWO TRICKS
##########################################################################
The director moves the body by writing entity fields, not by calling into the
AI. Four fields carry the whole death sequence and the reveal.

  frozen   1 = hold the current animation frame. The idle flap stops dead.
  shake    world units of jitter. >>> APPLY IT TO THE DRAW'S TRANSLATION
           ONLY, NEVER TO r->x/r->z. <<< Jittering the entity jitters its
           collision cylinder, its health bar and its light anchors with it;
           the body is meant to vibrate on the spot, not to be somewhere
           different every frame.
  fade     256 solid, 0 gone — see TRICK 1.
  clip_y   world Y below which polys are dropped — see TRICK 2.

>>> TRICK 1: "FADE AWAY" IS A BLEND MODE, NOT A COLOUR. <<<
A flat-shaded POLY_F4 has no alpha. Darkening the colour gives you a black
silhouette; fogging it toward the sky only works against the sky. Neither is a
fade. What works is ADDITIVE:

    setSemiTrans(poly, 1);
    /* colour scaled toward black by the fade */
    /* then, into the SAME OT bucket immediately AFTER the poly: */
    setDrawTPage(tp, 0, 0, getTPage(0, 1 /* ABR=1 */, 320, 0));

Additive black contributes nothing, so at fade 0 the body is genuinely gone
over ANY background. The OT is LIFO, which is why the tpage goes in AFTER the
poly it applies to — this is the same pattern lightswitch_puzzle.c's ls_quad
uses and the reason is worth re-reading there.

Use fade == 0 as a SKIP, not just an invisibility: it is also how the boss
stays out of the draw entirely while it waits under the floor, and it saves 476
polys a frame in both cases.

>>> TRICK 2: RISING THROUGH THE FLOOR NEEDS AN EXPLICIT CLIP. <<<
You cannot let the floor hide the buried half. Horizontal polys sort by their
FARTHEST corner (render.h's otz_far4), which pushes the floor BACK in the OT,
which means the still-underground model draws OVER the grass it is supposed to
be under. It looks like the boss is sliding up a wall in front of the lawn.

Drop the polys yourself. Convert the world cut plane into MODEL space once, so
the per-vertex test is one comparison and no transform — legal because neither
the yaw nor the lean changes a vertex's Y:

    clip_vy = clip_y - (r->y + <PFX>_FOOT_OFF);
    if (clipping && v0->vy > clip_vy && v1->vy > clip_vy && v2->vy > clip_vy &&
        (!is_quad || vp[vi[3]].vy > clip_vy)) { skip; }

Drop it only when EVERY corner is under the cut. Dropping anything that merely
straddles the plane eats a visible band off the silhouette; leaving the
stragglers lets a few small faces poke through the turf, which is the cheaper
error and — on something clawing out of the ground — the better-looking one.

Use a sentinel (RBS_NO_CLIP = 0x7FFFFFFF) for "off". Do not use 0: it is a
legal world Y, and a zero-filled struct would then arrive pre-clipped.

RISE LINEARLY, not eased. Five seconds of something hauling itself out of the
ground at a constant rate is the read you want; an ease-out looks like it is
being lifted on a wire. (The CAMERA still eases. Different thing, different
reason.)


##########################################################################
 STEP 6 — THE FIGHT: MOVEMENT, ATTACKS, PARRIES
##########################################################################
>>> EVERYTHING IN THE COMBAT CODE TARGETS player_x/y/z(), NEVER cam_*. <<<
The cutscenes anchor the player and fly the camera away. A boss that chases
cam_* aims its first fireball at a camera. This is the single most likely bug
in the whole feature and it is invisible until the cutscene runs.

MOVEMENT: STORE A PARAMETER, NOT A POSITION.
The Rabisu's `sweep` is a signed lateral offset in 4096ths, -4096 at one lip,
0 at the spawn, +4096 at the other. The world point is DERIVED each frame,
because the axis it is measured along is perpendicular to the player->spawn
line and therefore swings as the player moves.

"Moves side to side in an arc" means it holds its DISTANCE from the player, so
solve for the along-axis component rather than bending a straight line back
onto the circle:

    base  = |spawn - player|
    off   = (sweep * RADIUS) >> 12,  clamped to +/-base
    along = isqrt(base*base - off*off)
    pos   = player + unit(spawn-player)*along + perp(unit)*off

Doing it in that order is what keeps the ends of the sweep exactly ON the lips.
Normalising a straight-line target back onto the circle instead pulls the ends
IN — with the Rabisu's geometry, to x=816 instead of x=1142 — and the boss
quietly stops using a quarter of its arena.

Clamp the result to the arena bounds anyway. The arc is sized for the boss's
own spawn; the player is free to stand somewhere that swings it wide.

STOPPING POINTS. Pick from a fixed set, always different from the current one,
count the traversals, and stop when the quota is met:

    moves_target = MIN + rand() % (MAX - MIN + 1);   /* 3..8 */

Re-solve the arc point while STOPPED and while ATTACKING too. It is stationary
along its own arc, not pinned to a patch of grass, so it still tracks a player
who walks around it.

PROJECTILES. Model them on src/web.c — same straight-line flight, same
collision_segment_blocked() test on the SEGMENT JUST TRAVELLED (so a fast shot
cannot tunnel a thin wall between frames), same radial 3-axis contact test
(a flat XZ test counts a ball that sailed over the player's head).

>>> BEFORE YOU SHIP: CHECK WHAT THE ARENA'S SHORT WALLS DO TO A HITSCAN. <<<
collision_segment_blocked() only Y-gates walls in rooms flagged multi_level,
and most arenas are flat rooms where that flag is 0 — which means EVERY wall
blocks EVERY shot at EVERY height, including knee-high scenery.

If the arena's boundary is a low retaining lip (and if it is a good arena, it
probably is), that lip eats the boss's projectiles AND the player's bullets the
moment either of them stands behind it. The Rabisu fight only worked from the
middle of the lawn; from either terrace the fireballs died on a 100-unit step
and the Grave-olver could not touch the boss. Nothing on screen said why, and
it looks exactly like a range bug.

The fix already exists — shoot_over_mask, which the kitchen counter uses. Set
it from the room's *_init() AFTER the generated *_collision_init() (which
zeroes it), and set it by HEIGHT RULE, not by wall index:

    collision_shoot_over_short_walls(150);   /* in <room>_init() */

Indices are worthless here: the wall list is generated from the proxy mesh and
they shuffle on every re-export. A threshold that cleanly separates the steps
from the walls does not. Pick it by reading the y spans out of
src/<room>_mesh_collision.c — the courtyard's are 100 against 686 and 786, so
almost any threshold in between works and 150 is not a tuning knob.

Two things a web does not do:
  - SPEED IS SET BY THE FLIGHT TIME. Divide the whole distance by the frame
    count. The attack is specified as half a second regardless of range, and a
    parry window is only fair if the arrival is predictable.
  - IT CAN COME BACK. Give it an `owner` index and a `deflected` flag, and let
    the attack state end when the projectile is GONE rather than on a timer —
    that is what makes the attack "fully resolve" including the return leg.
    Keep a timer anyway as a backstop for the pool-was-full case, or the boss
    waits forever on a shot that never spawned.

>>> PARRIES: READ swing_timer DIRECTLY. <<<
crucifaxe.c counts swing_timer 1..SWING_TOTAL from the frame Square was
pressed. So "the player swung within N frames of this landing" is exactly:

    swing_timer > 0 && swing_timer <= N

evaluated on each frame of the final approach. It is non-zero only while the
axe is equipped, so no weapon check is needed. Note update_rabisus() runs
BEFORE weapons_update(), so the value is one frame stale — irrelevant against a
12-frame window, but know it.

A BOSS THE AXE CANNOT CUT still needs the axe to matter. Do not just delete the
melee path and leave the player with a dead button — make the swing the PARRY,
and say so where someone would look for the hit block:

  - remove the enemy's block from crucifaxe.c entirely, and leave a comment
    there explaining that the omission is the design, not an oversight;
  - remove <slug>s_try_hit() from the header too, with the same note. A stub
    that returns 0 just invites someone to fill it in.

Give the undodgeable attack the LONGER window. The Rabisu's foot slash re-aims
every frame (that is what "cannot be dodged" means mechanically), so the parry
is the only answer to it and gets 0.4 s against the fireball's 0.2 s.

>>> A PARRY WIDER THAN SWING_TOTAL IS ALWAYS-ON, AND IT WILL BE READ AS A
    BROKEN ATTACK. <<< SWING_TOTAL is 21 frames. A 24-frame window therefore
triggers on ANY swing in flight, not on a timed one — so a player who taps
Square out of habit blocks nearly every landing. The reported symptom was "the
foot slash almost never lands", which sounds like a range or aim bug and is
neither. If the brief asks for a window that wide, keep it and make the block
UNMISTAKABLE (below); if it does not, do not round upward.

MAKE A BLOCK LOOK DIFFERENT FROM A WHIFF. This is not polish. If a blocked hit
plays a small tick and nothing else, the player has no way to learn that the
parry exists, let alone that they are doing it — they conclude the attack is
buggy. Give it its own heavier sound and a reduced knockback, so "I stopped it"
and "it missed me" are never the same two frames.

>>> AND CHECK THE ATTACK PHYSICALLY REACHES. <<< Contact damage from a scripted
lunge is usually unconditional in code — the attack cannot be dodged, so the
landing always applies. That makes it very easy to ship a strike that deals its
damage from outside its own collision bubble, where nothing on screen touches
anything. Two numbers to check against the room, not against taste:

  - THE STANDOFF must be inside the push-out distance. rabisus_collide is
    called with a player radius of 75, so contact is RBS_BODY_RADIUS + 75 + 30
    = 360. An earlier 430 stopped the charge 70 clear of that: full damage,
    zero contact.
  - THE HEIGHT must be inside the frame. A hovering enemy holds its hover
    height unless you say otherwise, and the Rabisu's underside sits ON the
    terrace floor while the player's eye is 189 above it — so a "foot slash"
    swung its feet in below the bottom of the screen. Lerp the attacker's Y
    toward the player during the charge and back on the return.

SIZE THE SWEEP FOR THE FIGHT, NOT FOR THE ARENA. The Rabisu's arc was first
built to reach the lips exactly, because that was the number the brief handed
over — and at full reach the ends were clamped as often as they were reached
and the boss sprawled across the whole garden. It runs at 80% now. The lip is
still the boundary; the sweep just no longer touches it.

>>> AN ATTACK CAN BE A REACTION RATHER THAN A CHOICE. <<< Not everything has to
come out of the stop-and-pick pool. The Rabisu's shockwave fires because the
player walked inside its sweep radius: it abandons the arc, settles in the
middle and pulses every two seconds until they leave. That turns "get close" —
normally the safest place to be against a thing that circles you — into the
one place a wave passes through on a timer, and it costs one distance test in
two AI states.

Three things a zone reaction needs that a pool attack does not:
  - HYSTERESIS on the exit. Without it a player standing exactly on the radius
    flips the boss between centring and sweeping every single frame.
  - THE TEST MEASURED FROM THE ARENA CENTRE, not from the boss. The boss is the
    thing that moves to the middle, so measuring from it drags the zone around
    behind it.
  - THE WAVE TICKED OUTSIDE THE AI SWITCH, so one already in the air keeps
    expanding after the boss has gone back to sweeping — or died. Launching it
    was the commitment; escaping it afterwards should not be possible just
    because the state changed.

A TELEGRAPHED PATH ATTACK (the light beam: chest glows 1.5 s, then a line of
ground polys ignites one at a time toward the player) has two traps of its own:
  - DRAW THE PATH WHEN THE CHARGE ENDS, NOT WHEN IT STARTS. A path locked in at
    the start of the tell is aimed at where the player stood 1.5 s before the
    first poly lit, which makes the tell pointless.
  - FREEZE THE ORIGIN. The boss keeps re-solving its arc position while the
    walk plays, so a path derived from its LIVE position swings around as the
    player circles. Store both endpoints at draw time.
  - LAY THE CELLS OUT BACKWARD FROM THE PLAYER. Stepping forward from the boss
    at a fixed pitch leaves the last cell wherever the division lands; stepping
    back from the player puts it exactly on them, which is the requirement.
  - Store the path as TWO ENDPOINTS AND A COUNT, not as a list. It is a
    straight line at a fixed pitch, so any cell is a lerp — and fourteen stored
    pairs would be 112 bytes per instance in the save blob for three lines of
    arithmetic.

A LEAN, OR ANY SECOND ROTATION, must be composed INSIDE the yaw:

    MulMatrix0(&yawm, &leanm, &pm);      /* pm = yaw * lean */

and built BY HAND, not through RotMatrix — because <slug>_anchor_world() has to
reproduce the identical transform (STEP 7), and two hand-written matrices
cannot disagree about a sign convention the way a hand-written one and
RotMatrix can. Expose the lean's direction as a one-line flip constant, the
same way the facing already is.


##########################################################################
 STEP 7 — LIGHT
##########################################################################
>>> PUT THE PRIMITIVES IN THE ENEMY FILE, NOT THE DIRECTOR. <<< It is natural
to write them where the first user is, which is the reveal — and wrong, because
the boss's own ATTACKS end up needing them too, and an attack has to work for a
second one of these dropped somewhere with no cutscene attached. The Rabisu's
four glow users are the reveal's lawn lights, the death's crown/wing/chest
lights, the light beam's burning path and the shockwave; two of the four are
attacks. They live in rabisu.c as rbs_glow_pulse / _quad / _point / _pillar and
rabisu_boss.c calls them. Moving them afterwards is easy but pointless work.

The clock they share (rbs_glow_clock) belongs there too, ticked once per
update_<slug>s(). Two free-running clocks in two files drift.

Kinds of light, one colour ramp. Sharing the ramp is the point: if the death
and the attacks are meant to read as the same phenomenon as the reveal, the
colours have to BE the same colours.

    static const uint8_t PULSE[3][3] = { white, orange, red };
    leg = (t / STEP) % 3;  f = ((t % STEP) * 256) / STEP;
    colour = lerp(PULSE[leg], PULSE[(leg+1)%3], f)  scaled by a 0..256 level

Give each lamp its own clock offset (`pulse_t + cell*9`) or the whole bank
strobes as one lamp.

FADING AN ADDITIVE POLY MEANS SCALING TOWARD BLACK. There is nothing to blend
toward, so the usual fog-colour lerp does not apply — distance fade, brightness
and the master fade are all just multiplies on the level.

A. GROUND LIGHTS are lightswitch_puzzle.c's cones, minus the cube. A pool quad
   sitting a few units above the floor poly (or it z-fights), plus a shaft
   above it. Band the shaft along its length so no single quad is large enough
   to blow past the GTE's +/-1023 screen clamp if the player ever stands in one.

   >>> WHICH END OF THE CONE IS WIDE IS THE WHOLE READ. <<< The lightswitch
   cones hang from ceiling cubes and NARROW downward onto the floor, because
   they are lamps shining down. Copy that geometry for light coming OUT OF the
   ground and you get exactly the same picture — a spotlight in the sky picking
   out each poly — no matter what the beat sheet says it is. Light emanating
   from the ground FLARES as it rises: push each corner AWAY from its cell's
   centre with height, not toward it. It is one sign, and it inverts the
   meaning of the whole effect.

   Shade it along its length too. A beam at a single brightness top to bottom
   reads as a solid box; falling off with height puts the source at the ground,
   which is the other half of the same read. Band count exists for the screen
   clamp anyway, so the shading is free.

   Keep the shaft FAINT (~60/256) and the pool BRIGHT (~210/256). Sixteen
   overlapping beams at pool brightness white out the middle of the arena.

   "Two at a time over 3 seconds" — spread the eight start times over
   (3 s - one fade) rather than the full three seconds, so the last pair
   finishes coming up exactly as the phase ends. Spread them over the full
   duration and the last pair is still at three-quarter brightness when the
   next phase starts, and pops.

   Light them in a deliberate ORDER. The Rabisu's pairs each cell with its
   diagonal opposite so the patch opens outward from the middle — which is
   where the thing is about to come up — instead of filling in from a corner.

B. LIGHTS ON THE MODEL are glow sprites hung on mesh-local anchors.

   Get the anchors from the animation, not the bind pose, and from the FRAME
   THE DEATH FREEZES ON so they are exact rather than average. Dump the .pva:
   the highest cluster of vertices is the crown, the extreme +/-X are the wing
   tips, the centre-line front face is the chest. Name them in the header as
   <PFX>_A_* constants with the reasoning beside them.

   Turn a local anchor into a world point through <slug>_anchor_world(), which
   MUST apply lean, then yaw, then translate + FOOT_OFF — the same order and
   the same matrices as the draw. That contract is the whole reason the lights
   stay stuck to the head.

   Draw each as concentric additive SCREEN-space squares around the projected
   point (the same explicit-projection trick the health bar uses, since a model
   has no billboard quad to hang anything off). Halve the size and raise the
   level per ring: three rings give a soft falloff with a hot core, for no
   texture, no VRAM and no sorting.

   Size from the WORLD distance — half = world_half * 256 / dist, matching
   gte_SetGeomScreen(256) — so the glow is the same physical size whatever the
   shot. Clamp dist away from 0.

   RAMP THE LIGHT ON over the phase and then let it ride the model's own fade
   out, so the light never outlives the body it is pouring out of.

C. AN EXPANDING RING (the shockwave) is wedges around an arc, and it wants BOTH
   a band on the ground and a short vertical skirt on its leading edge. The
   band says where the wave is; the skirt is the part the player can actually
   see coming, because in first person a flat figure on the floor vanishes the
   moment it is more than a few metres out. Twenty segments is plenty.

   >>> LOOK THE FLOOR HEIGHT UP PER CORNER. <<< A wave crossing an arena with
   terraces in it — and a good arena has terraces in it — is drawn at one Y
   only if you let it, and then it sinks into two thirds of the room. One
   floor-zone lookup per corner is four scans of a seven-entry list; it is not
   the expensive part of anything.

   Give the whole ring ONE clock, unlike the lawn lights' per-cell offsets. It
   is a single object, and shimmering its segments out of phase breaks it into
   twenty separate flickers.


##########################################################################
 STEP 8 — TEXT
##########################################################################
btn_prompt_draw(ctx, x, y, s, ot_idx) is the screen-space text you want. It
sorts via FntSort — never FntFlush, which draws IMMEDIATELY and races a scene
still being laid down (see the note on main.c's debug overlay).

The debug font advances 8 px a character on a 320-wide screen, so FORTY
CHARACTERS IS THE HARD LIMIT. Both of the Rabisu's lines are past it. Wrap them
BY HAND at their own commas and full stops — an automatic wrapper will break
"Thou art but a servant. The cursed" in the wrong place — and centre each row
with strlen*8.

Sort into the menu-reserved range (OT 0..15) so the lines sit on top of
everything, and draw them from the room's draw AFTER the world.


##########################################################################
 STEP 9 — MUSIC, THE SEAL, AND THE INPUT LOCK
##########################################################################
MUSIC. If the arena track is the boss's track, take it off the room entry in
main.c's STATE_LOADING branch and start it from the phase machine at the beat
the brief names. Replace it with cdaudio_stop(), NOT with nothing: a
title-screen load or a debug level-select jump into that room does not pass
through the door transition's own stop, and would arrive with the previous
room's music still playing. Stop it again on the kill.

THE SEAL. Test it BEFORE the door trigger, so the trigger is never polled:

    if (!lock && !<slug>_boss_seals_door() && <room>_door_triggered()) { ... }

and call <room>_door_arm() at the moment the seal LIFTS. The trigger routines
hold their own Circle edge state, and one that has not been polled for four
minutes is stale — without the re-arm the player's first press after the fight
is swallowed or, worse, a press held through the whole death sequence fires
instantly.

Suppress the door's floating prompt for the same span. Offering "Press O to
enter" on a door that will not answer is worse than offering nothing.

>>> BEFORE PROMISING A SEALED ARENA, CHECK THE ROOM HAS NO SAVE POINT. <<<
Sealing the door is only a real seal if there is no other way out of the state.
The Garden Courtyard calls save_points_clear() and places none of its own, so
the only exits are winning and dying — which is what makes "no re-entry
handling needed" true rather than wishful.

THE INPUT LOCK. Add an early-return branch in update_current_area(), beside the
stove and lightswitch payoffs, gated on the CUTSCENE predicate (not the seal —
the fight in the middle is free play and must fall through):

    if (area == <ROOM> && <slug>_boss_cutscene()) {
        update_<slug>s();        /* it keeps facing the player throughout */
        player_status_update();
        <slug>_boss_update();
        update_particles();
        return;                  /* no update_camera, no collision, no doors */
    }

Skipping update_camera is also what keeps the crane shot at its own height:
apply_height() would otherwise drag cam_y down to the floor.

Add the same predicate to main.c's `puzzle` flag so the Start menu and the
weapon overlay are suppressed. Unlike the puzzles, ALSO suppress the HUD:
nothing can touch the player during a reveal or a death, and a health bar over
them is a frame of UI insisting on a fight that is not on.

##########################################################################
 STEP 11 — DEATH IS TWO FLAGS, NOT ONE
##########################################################################
>>> `dead` ALREADY MEANS "SKIPPED BY EVERYTHING". A DEATH SEQUENCE NEEDS THE
    BODY TO STAY DRAWN. <<<
Setting the existing flag on the killing blow blinks the boss out of existence
at the exact moment the player is supposed to watch it come apart.

Add a second flag and split the meanings:

  dying   set by <slug>_damage() when health hits 0. Stops the AI, the
          collision cylinder, the health bar and every weapon. The body is
          STILL DRAWN. The director watches for this to start the sequence.
  dead    set by the DIRECTOR at the very end of the fade. Gone: skipped by
          update, draw, the gun, and world_enter's placement.

Then audit every `if (... || r->dead ...) continue;` in the codebase and decide
which of them also wants `|| r->dying`. The answer for the Rabisu:

  update loop     no  — it keeps facing the player while it dies
  draw loop       no  — that is the entire point
  health bar      YES — an empty bar hanging over the death sequence is the
                        one piece of HUD still insisting there is a fight on
  collide         YES — and it MATTERS: the death sequence walks the body back
                        to its spawn, which would shove the player across the
                        arena if the cylinder were still live
  graveolver      YES — a round spent into a corpse should miss

In the emulator, in this order, because each depends on the last:

##########################################################################
 MISTAKES ACTUALLY MADE, IN ORDER OF HOW LONG THEY WOULD HAVE TAKEN TO FIND
##########################################################################
1. THE YAW-ONLY VIEW MATRIX ATE THE CRANE SHOT'S PITCH.
   Two places, both needing the same one-line fix, and the failure has no error
   and no visual clue beyond "the shot is framed wrong". camera.h documents it;
   nobody reads camera.h while writing a cutscene. STEP 3.

2. THE ARENA'S OWN SCENERY ATE EVERY PROJECTILE CROSSING IT.
   A flat room does not Y-gate hitscans, so the 100-unit retaining lip that
   DEFINES the arena blocked fireballs and bullets alike at any height. The
   fight worked from the middle of the lawn and nowhere else, and the symptom
   ("the fireballs don't reach me over here") points at range, aim, lifetime
   and speed — everything except a wall you cannot see from eye level.
   STEP 6, shoot_over_mask.

3. NORMALISING THE ARC TARGET INSTEAD OF SOLVING FOR IT.
   Bending a straight-line offset back onto the player's circle looks like the
   same thing and is not: it pulls the ends of the sweep in, and the boss
   silently stops using a quarter of the arena it was given. STEP 6.

4. RE-LOOKING-UP THE BOSS EVERY FRAME OF A RUNNING SCRIPT.
   Two bugs from one line. The encounter killed its own final camera move the
   frame after it set `dead`, dumping the player in the middle of the garden —
   reported, reasonably, as "it put me back at the start of the fight". And
   opening the inventory menu, which changes game_state, ended the whole
   encounter and unsealed the door. STEP 4.

5. A LUNGE THAT DEALT ITS DAMAGE FROM OUTSIDE ITS OWN COLLISION BUBBLE, AT
   ANKLE HEIGHT, AND THEN GOT BLOCKED BY AN IDLE SWING.
   Three separate causes, one symptom — "the foot slash almost never lands" —
   and none of them is what that sentence sounds like. The damage was
   unconditional the whole time. STEP 6.

6. ONE `dead` FLAG FOR A NINE-SECOND DEATH.
   STEP 11.

7. THE CONE POINTED THE WRONG WAY UP.
   Inherited wholesale from the lightswitch puzzle, whose cones hang from the
   CEILING. One sign, and it turns "light pouring out of the ground" into
   "spotlight shining onto the ground". STEP 7A.

8. THE BOSS SPENT ITS REVEAL LOOKING AT THE DOOR.
   Correct code — it targets the player, and the player is anchored by the door
   during a cutscene. STEP 3.

9. STOPPING SHORT OF THE PLAYER-ANCHOR RULE.
   Tempting to leave the existing cam_* targeting alone because it works during
   the fight — and it does. It only breaks during the cutscenes, which are the
   part you test last. STEP 6.

10. HANDING CONTROL BACK ON A PUSH-OUT BOUNDARY.
   -1740 is exactly GC_WALL_RADIUS from the south wall, and "exactly" means the
   first frame of free play may shove the player. Sit short of it. STEP 2E.
